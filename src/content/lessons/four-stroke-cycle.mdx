steps:
  - title: Two revolutions, four strokes
    body: A four-stroke engine takes two full crankshaft revolutions to complete one cycle per cylinder. That is why the camshafts turn at exactly half crank speed. Play the simulation slowly and watch one piston.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.piston
  - title: Intake
    body: The piston descends with the intake valve open, drawing in air. On a port-injected engine the fuel joins here.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.valve_intake
  - title: Compression
    body: Both valves shut and the piston rises, compressing the charge. Compressing it also heats it, which is what makes a diesel ignite without a spark at all.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.piston_rings
  - title: Power
    body: Ignition happens slightly before top dead centre so that peak pressure arrives just after the piston starts down. That timing is the single biggest lever on efficiency and on knock.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.spark_plug
  - title: Exhaust
    body: The exhaust valve opens and the rising piston pushes burnt gas out. On a turbocharged engine that gas still holds enough energy to drive a compressor.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.valve_exhaust
  - title: Why the crank is the reference
    body: Every event above is defined by crank angle, not by time. That is why the timing chain matters so much - it is what keeps the valve events tied to the piston positions.
    system: powertrain
    focus: powertrain.crankshaft
---

The cycle every combustion engine runs, tied to the one thing that defines it: crank angle.
