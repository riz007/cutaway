A short reinforced rubber hose bridging the body to each wheel assembly, where the rigid <Part id="braking.hard_line" /> cannot follow.

## What it does

It allows the movement a hard line cannot. A front wheel steers through a wide arc and travels the full suspension stroke, and the hose has to accommodate both, thousands of times, without kinking, chafing or splitting.

Its construction is a compromise. Rubber alone would balloon under 100 bar, so it is reinforced with braided layers that limit expansion while allowing bending. Some expansion remains, and it is accounted for in the pedal feel the manufacturer designs around — which is why braided steel hoses give a firmer pedal.

The hose is deliberately routed with a specific twist and clip position so that it neither pulls tight at full droop nor rubs on the tyre at full lock.

## How it fails

Perishing and cracking of the outer cover with age, ozone and heat. Internal delamination is the dangerous one: a flap of liner can act as a one-way valve, letting pressure through to apply the brake but not releasing it, giving one wheel that drags and overheats.

## How it is serviced

Inspected at every brake service. Replaced by age as much as by mileage.
