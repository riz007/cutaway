The run from tank to engine bay: mostly rigid nylon or steel tube clipped along the floor, with short flexible sections at each end where the powertrain moves relative to the body.

## What it does

It carries fuel at supply pressure without leaking, without permeating vapour through its walls, and without absorbing enough heat to boil the fuel inside. Routing is deliberate — away from exhaust heat, above the lowest point of the underbody so it is not the first thing a kerb strikes, and clipped at intervals so it cannot chafe.

Quick-connect fittings at the ends make assembly fast at the factory and are the usual place a leak appears years later.

<Market region="Thailand">
Ethanol and biodiesel both attack elastomers that mineral fuels leave alone. With E20 petrol and B7 diesel as the standard grades, line and seal materials have to be rated for them — a consideration for older imports and for any aftermarket fuel plumbing.
</Market>

## How it fails

Corrosion on steel sections, chafing where a clip has broken, and hardened or cracked quick-connect O-rings.

<Warning>
The line stays pressurised after shutdown. It must be depressurised before any fitting is opened.
</Warning>

## How it is serviced

Inspected visually for damp patches and corrosion. Not a replacement item on interval.
