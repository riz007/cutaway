The structural and drainage channel at the base of the windscreen, hidden under the plastic scuttle panel and the wiper arms.

## What it does

Three jobs at once. Structurally it is a closed box section spanning the full width of the car, tying the two A-pillars together and stiffening the top of the <Part id="structure.firewall" />. It is a large part of the body's torsional stiffness and of how solid the car feels.

It manages water. Rain running off the windscreen collects here, along with leaves and grit, and must be led away through drains at each end and out under the car. This is the vehicle's principal water management feature and the one most often defeated by debris.

And it is the fresh air intake. The base of the windscreen is a high-pressure zone at speed, so it naturally forces air into the HVAC system. That is why the <Part id="hvac.cabin_filter" /> generally lives just behind it.

## How it fails

Blocked drains, and it is a consequential failure: water backs up in the cowl and overflows into the HVAC housing and the passenger footwell, or seeps through firewall grommets.

<Market region="Thailand">
Heavy rain and leaf litter make cowl drains a genuine maintenance item. A wet passenger carpet after a downpour is very often blocked cowl drains rather than a door seal.
</Market>

## How it is serviced

Drains cleared periodically, especially where a car parks under trees.
