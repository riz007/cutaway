A moulded plastic box holding the air filter, with an inlet snorkel drawing from a chosen point in the engine bay and an outlet duct to the throttle.

## What it does

Three jobs, only one of which is obvious. It houses and seals the <Part id="air.air_filter" />, so all intake air is filtered air. It draws from cool, clean, splash-free air — usually high up behind the headlight rather than from the hot bay itself, because cooler air is denser.

And it tunes noise. The box volume and snorkel length form a Helmholtz resonator, cancelling specific intake frequencies. That is why intake ducts have odd bulges and side branches hanging off them: they are acoustic devices, not styling.

<Note>
Replacing the airbox with an open cone filter usually makes an engine slower, not faster. It gains a little flow and loses the cold-air feed, the resonator tuning, and a properly sealed filter.
</Note>

<Market region="Thailand">
Dust and heavy wet-season rain make the sealing and the water-shedding path of the airbox matter more than in temperate markets. A mis-seated lid after a filter change lets unfiltered dust straight into the engine.
</Market>

## How it fails

Cracked housings and lids that no longer seal, and split ducting downstream of the <Part id="air.maf_sensor" />, which causes a lean condition.

## How it is serviced

Cleaned out when the filter is changed. Check the lid clips.
