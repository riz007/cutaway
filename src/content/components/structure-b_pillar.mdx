The pillar between the front and rear doors, running from the sill to the roof rail. Structurally it is the backbone of side impact protection.

## What it does

It ties the floor to the roof at the vehicle's midpoint, which is where a side impact usually lands. Load from a struck door passes through the <Part id="structure.side_impact_bar" /> into the pillar, and the pillar distributes it up into the roof and down into the sill and floor cross-members.

It is engineered with a deliberate strength gradient. The upper section is extremely strong, so it does not fold inwards at chest and head height. The lower section is designed to be more yielding, so that the pillar bends inward at hip level rather than collapsing at the top — moving the intrusion to the part of the body best able to tolerate it. Achieving that means using different steel grades, or different tempering, along a single stamped part.

It also carries the front seat belt reel and upper anchorage, the door latch striker and the rear door hinges.

## How it fails

Only in a severe side impact.

## How it is serviced

Never. Any pillar deformation is a structural repair, and the seat belt anchorage integrity must be verified.
