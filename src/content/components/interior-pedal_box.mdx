The bracket mounting the pedals to the <Part id="structure.firewall" />, and one of the more thoughtfully engineered assemblies in the cabin.

## What it does

It provides the pivots and the leverage ratios. The <Part id="braking.pedal" />'s ratio is chosen with the <Part id="braking.booster" /> to give a specific relationship between foot force and deceleration, and its pivot position sets how the pedal moves under the foot — a subtle contribution to how progressive the brakes feel.

Pedal spacing and height are set by regulation and ergonomics. Heel-and-toe use aside, the practical requirement is that a driver cannot accidentally catch two pedals at once, and that the foot can move between them quickly without lifting.

Its crash behaviour is deliberate. In a frontal impact the firewall moves rearward, and a rigidly mounted pedal would be driven into the driver's foot and ankle — a common source of serious lower-limb injury. Modern pedal boxes are designed to release, deform or rotate the pedal away at a set load, decoupling it from the intruding structure.

On drive-by-wire vehicles the accelerator is simply a sprung pedal with two redundant position sensors.

## How it fails

Worn pivot bushes giving lost motion, and dry pivots causing a creak.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Floor mats must never be allowed to interfere with pedal travel.
