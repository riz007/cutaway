The pillar running from the cowl up each side of the windscreen to the roof. It is usually made from the strongest steel in the vehicle.

## What it does

It is the front corner of the passenger safety cell, and it does the most in the least favourable situation: a rollover. With the car inverted, the A-pillars carry a large share of the vehicle's weight, and regulations require the roof structure to withstand several times the vehicle's mass without significant crush. That requirement, more than any other, drives the use of hot-formed boron steel here.

It also anchors the windscreen, which is itself structural — a bonded laminated screen contributes meaningfully to body stiffness and to keeping the roof up in a rollover.

The unavoidable tension is visibility. A pillar strong enough for the rollover requirement is thick enough to hide a pedestrian at a junction, and raked windscreens make the blind spot longer. Manufacturers narrow it with higher-grade steels and split-pillar designs, but it cannot be eliminated.

## How it fails

Only in a severe impact. It cannot be repaired by heating and straightening, because that destroys the properties of hot-formed steel.

## How it is serviced

Never. Its integrity is assessed after any roof or front-corner damage.
