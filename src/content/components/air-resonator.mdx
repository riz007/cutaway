A chamber in the exhaust that removes specific frequencies rather than reducing noise generally. It works by interference, not absorption.

## What it does

An exhaust note is not one sound but a set of frequencies determined by cylinder count and engine speed. Some are pleasant; one or two are the low-frequency drone that becomes intolerable at a steady motorway cruise.

A resonator is tuned to those. In a Helmholtz design, gas in a side branch of a specific volume and neck length oscillates at a chosen frequency and, being out of phase with the incoming wave, cancels it. In a quarter-wave design, a closed stub of a specific length reflects a wave back so it arrives inverted.

Because the treatment is frequency-selective rather than broadband, the exhaust can be quiet at cruise without sounding strangled everywhere else. Intake resonators on the <Part id="air.airbox" /> do the same job on the other side of the engine.

## How it fails

Internal corrosion and cracked welds. The symptom is a droning boom at one particular speed.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item; replaced when corroded.
