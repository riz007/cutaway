The radiator is a heat exchanger between two fluids: hot coolant on the inside, moving air on the outside. Roughly a third of the energy in the fuel an engine burns leaves through it as waste heat.

## What it does

Coolant arrives hot through the <Part id="cooling.upper_hose" />, spreads across a wide, thin core of tubes and fins, and gives up heat to air passing between them. It leaves through the <Part id="cooling.lower_hose" /> to the <Part id="cooling.water_pump" /> inlet.

Airflow comes from road speed at pace and from the <Part id="cooling.cooling_fan" /> when stationary. The <Part id="hvac.condenser" /> usually sits ahead of it in the same stack, which is why air conditioning makes an engine run slightly hotter in traffic.

<Note>
The system is pressurised, to about one bar above atmospheric. That is not incidental — it raises the boiling point of the coolant by roughly twenty-five degrees, which is what stops localised boiling in the hottest passages of the cylinder head.
</Note>

## How it fails

External blockage from leaves and insects, internal blockage from old degraded coolant, and physical damage from stones are all common. Plastic end tanks become brittle with age and split at the crimped seam.

<Warning>
Never open a hot cooling system. The coolant is above its atmospheric boiling point and will flash to steam the moment pressure is released.
</Warning>

## How it is serviced

Coolant replaced on the manufacturer's interval, using the specified type — mixing incompatible coolants can gel. Rinse the exterior of the core when washing the car.
