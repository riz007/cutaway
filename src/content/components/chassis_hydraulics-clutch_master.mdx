A small hydraulic cylinder on the firewall, turning clutch pedal movement into fluid pressure and displacement.

## What it does

Hydraulics replaced the clutch cable for reasons beyond convenience. A cable has friction that varies with its routing and its age, so pedal feel changes over time and with temperature. Fluid does not, so a hydraulic clutch feels the same on day one and year ten.

It is also self-adjusting. A cable stretches and the clutch wears, so a cable system needs periodic free-play adjustment; a hydraulic system simply takes a little more fluid from the reservoir as the clutch wears, exactly as brakes do.

Bore sizing sets the trade-off between pedal effort and travel, and matching the master bore to the <Part id="chassis_hydraulics.clutch_slave" /> bore determines how much clutch release movement a given pedal stroke produces.

It shares the brake fluid reservoir on most vehicles, which is why clutch problems can appear after brake work and why the fluid specification is the same.

## How it fails

Internal seal wear, which lets fluid bypass the piston. The symptom is a pedal that goes to the floor and gear selection that becomes difficult, particularly when everything is hot.

## How it is serviced

Fluid changed with the brake fluid. Not otherwise serviceable.
