A translucent plastic tank on top of the <Part id="braking.master_cylinder" />, usually with separate internal compartments feeding the two independent circuits.

## What it does

It keeps the master cylinder supplied and accommodates volume changes in the system. The fluid level falls slowly and normally as the friction material wears: as pads thin, the caliper pistons sit further out, and that displaced volume comes from the reservoir. A reservoir at minimum with worn pads is expected; a reservoir at minimum with new pads means a leak.

The divided compartments are a safety feature. A leak that empties one circuit leaves fluid in the other, so the car retains braking on two wheels.

The cap breathes through a labyrinth or diaphragm, which is where the system's biggest chemical problem enters.

<Market region="Thailand">
Brake fluid is hygroscopic — it absorbs water from the air. High year-round humidity accelerates this, and absorbed water dramatically lowers the boiling point. Fluid that boils on a long descent gives a pedal that goes to the floor. Two-year fluid changes are not optional maintenance in this climate.
</Market>

## How it fails

Cracked plastic, a perished cap seal, and level sensor faults.

## How it is serviced

Level checked at every service; fluid replaced roughly every two years, or by moisture test.
