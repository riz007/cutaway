The whole propulsion system of an electric car, in one sealed housing with no gearbox, clutch, exhaust or cooling fan of its own.

## What it does

It produces torque from a rotating magnetic field in the stator dragging the rotor around with it. The <Part id="electrical.inverter" /> creates that field and controls its strength and phase, so the inverter — not the motor — is where the intelligence lives.

The torque curve is the reason an EV feels the way it does. Full torque is available from a standstill and stays flat until base speed, after which it tapers to hold constant power. A combustion engine produces almost nothing below a thousand rpm and needs a gearbox to hide that; an electric motor needs one fixed <Part id="drivetrain.reduction_gear" /> and nothing else.

It is also bidirectional, so the same machine recovers energy under braking.

<Warning>
High voltage. The motor generates voltage whenever the wheels turn. A vehicle being towed with driven wheels on the ground can energise its own system.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Bearing wear and, rarely, insulation or coolant-seal failure. Motors typically outlast the pack.

## How it is serviced

Coolant on schedule. The unit itself is not user-serviceable.
