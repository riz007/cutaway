The return hose, carrying cooled coolant from the bottom of the radiator back to the <Part id="cooling.water_pump" /> inlet.

## What it does

The same conducting job as the upper hose, under a specific extra load: it is on the suction side of the pump. When the pump is spinning hard at high rpm, it can pull the pressure inside this hose below atmospheric, and a plain rubber tube would simply collapse shut.

That is why lower hoses often contain a wire helix bonded into the wall. It contributes nothing to pressure containment and everything to resisting collapse under suction.

A collapsed lower hose produces a distinctive fault: the engine cools normally at low speed and overheats at sustained high rpm, because that is exactly when suction is greatest.

## How it fails

Internal collapse when the support spring corrodes or the rubber softens, plus the usual age hardening and cracking. Because it sits low and is easily reached by road spray and debris, it also suffers more external damage than the upper hose.

## How it is serviced

Inspected with the upper hose. Both are normally replaced together, since they have had identical service lives.
