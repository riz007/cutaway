A small electric motor and gearbox bolted to each rear caliper, replacing the cable and lever with a switch.

## What it does

It winds the caliper piston out mechanically and holds it there. Once applied, the gearing is self-locking, so no current is needed to keep the car held — the brake stays on with the battery disconnected.

The advantages over a cable are not mainly convenience. It applies with a repeatable, calibrated force every time rather than depending on how hard the driver pulled. It frees up the console space a handbrake lever occupied. And because it is an actuator the vehicle can command, it enables hill-hold, automatic application when the engine stops, and release timed to clutch engagement.

Because the piston is driven by a screw rather than hydraulics, the actuator must be put into service mode with a scan tool before pads are replaced, and the piston wound back the same way.

## How it fails

Motor and gear failure, and corrosion at the connector. A seized mechanism can leave the brake applied.

## How it is serviced

No routine service, but it must be retracted electronically for pad work — forcing it back mechanically destroys it.
