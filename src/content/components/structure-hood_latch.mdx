A two-stage catch holding the bonnet closed. The two stages are the whole safety argument for its design.

## What it does

The primary latch is released from inside the cabin. If that were all, a cable pulled accidentally, or a latch that failed, would let the bonnet fly up at speed and completely block the driver's view — one of the more dangerous failures a car can have.

So there is a secondary catch, a hook that engages independently and must be released by hand at the front of the car. Both have to be defeated for the bonnet to open, and they are released in different ways from different places. A bonnet that pops to the safety catch at speed is alarming but survivable.

The latch mechanism includes a switch on many vehicles, so the instrument cluster can warn that the bonnet is not fully closed.

Some vehicles add an active bonnet that lifts a few centimetres on pedestrian impact, creating clearance above the hard engine components.

## How it fails

Corrosion and dried grease making it stiff, a stretched or seized release cable, and a bent striker that leaves the bonnet sitting proud.

## How it is serviced

Cleaned and lubricated at service. The secondary catch should be checked for free movement — it is the one that matters.
