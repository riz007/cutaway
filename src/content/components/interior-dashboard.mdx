A large moulded assembly on a steel cross-car beam, carrying the instruments, vents, controls, passenger airbag and the HVAC case behind it.

## What it does

The visible plastic is the least interesting part. Behind it runs the cross-car beam — a steel or magnesium tube from one A-pillar to the other — which is a genuine structural member. It stiffens the body across its widest unsupported span, carries the steering column so the wheel does not shake, and provides the reaction point the passenger airbag pushes against when it fires.

The dashboard's upper surface is shaped and textured to control windscreen reflections, which is why it is matt and dark on almost every car.

It is also a significant safety surface. Everything within reach of an occupant's head must meet head impact criteria, so hard points are padded or moved, and switch shapes are designed to break away rather than penetrate.

Behind it sits the HVAC case with the <Part id="hvac.evaporator" /> and <Part id="cooling.heater_core" />, which is why heater core replacement is such a large job.

## How it fails

Rattles and squeaks as clips age, and warping or cracking of the top surface.
