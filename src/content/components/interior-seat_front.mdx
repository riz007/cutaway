A structural component that happens to be comfortable. In a crash it is part of the restraint system, not furniture.

## What it does

It positions the occupant correctly relative to the <Part id="safety_adas.airbag_front" /> and the belt geometry. An airbag is calibrated for a person in a particular place; a seat too far forward or reclined too far puts them outside that design envelope, which is why seating position is a safety setting rather than a preference.

Its frame anchors the belt buckle and, on most cars, the side airbag, so it must resist the full restraint load. It also has to prevent submarining — the occupant sliding forward under the lap belt — which is why the cushion pan is shaped with a rise at the front and often contains an anti-submarining ramp.

The head restraint is a crash device. Its purpose is to limit rearward head travel in a rear impact and so prevent whiplash, which means it must be set with its top level with the top of the head, not low behind the neck.

## How it fails

Worn foam changing the seating position, failed recliner mechanisms, and seat heater element breaks. Occupant detection mats in the passenger seat can fail and disable the passenger airbag.

## How it is serviced

Rails cleaned and lubricated. Any seat with a deployed side airbag is replaced or rebuilt to specification.
