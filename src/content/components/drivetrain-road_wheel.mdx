The only parts of the vehicle that touch the road. Every force that accelerates, stops or turns the car passes through four contact patches roughly the size of a hand.

## What it does

The tyre generates grip through two mechanisms: adhesion, a molecular attraction between rubber and road, and hysteresis, where the tread deforms around surface texture and loses energy doing so. Hysteresis is why a wet road still offers grip and why tyre compound matters more than tread pattern on dry tarmac.

The tread pattern's real job is water. On a wet road the tyre must clear the water film ahead of the contact patch, and the grooves are drainage channels. Their volume, not their appearance, is what resists aquaplaning — which is why tread depth matters overwhelmingly in the wet and hardly at all in the dry.

The tyre is also the vehicle's first spring and damper, absorbing small road inputs the suspension is too coarse to catch. Sidewall height sets how much of that compliance exists.

## How it fails

Wear, ageing, sidewall damage from kerbs and potholes, and puncture.

## How it is serviced

Pressures checked monthly when cold; rotated and inspected at service.
