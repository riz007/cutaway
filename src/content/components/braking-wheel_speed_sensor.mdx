A small sensor at each hub reading a toothed or magnetically encoded ring that turns with the wheel. It is one of the most widely used signals in the whole vehicle.

## What it does

It reports how fast each wheel is turning, individually. From those four numbers a remarkable amount is derived.

The <Part id="braking.abs_modulator" /> compares each wheel against the others to detect a wheel decelerating far faster than the vehicle can be — the signature of a wheel about to lock. Traction control watches for the opposite. Stability control combines them with the <Part id="safety_adas.yaw_sensor" /> to work out whether the car is following the path the steering asked for. The speedometer, odometer, cruise control and tyre pressure monitoring all read the same signals.

Modern sensors are active — powered, using a Hall effect element — rather than passive, which lets them read down to walking pace and even detect direction of rotation. That is what makes hill-hold and rollback detection possible.

## How it fails

Corroded connectors, damaged cables from stone strikes, and debris on the encoder ring. One faulty sensor typically disables ABS, stability control and cruise together, illuminating several lights at once.

## How it is serviced

Not routine. The ring is cleaned when the hub is disturbed.
