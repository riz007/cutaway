The receiving end of the clutch hydraulic circuit, converting pressure back into the movement that releases the <Part id="drivetrain.clutch" />.

## What it does

It pushes the release bearing against the diaphragm spring fingers, which levers the pressure plate away from the friction disc and disconnects the engine from the gearbox.

There are two arrangements. An external slave sits on the bellhousing and operates a fork through a pushrod, which is accessible and easy to replace. A concentric slave — increasingly common — is an annular cylinder that sits around the gearbox input shaft inside the bellhousing, with the release bearing built onto it.

The concentric design is neater, lighter and has no fork linkage to wear or misalign. Its drawback is decisive when it fails: replacing it means separating the gearbox from the engine, so it is always replaced together with the clutch even if the clutch has life left. Fitting a new clutch and reusing the old concentric slave is a false economy that most workshops will refuse.

## How it fails

Seal wear causing an internal or external leak. A concentric unit leaks into the bellhousing, where the fluid contaminates the clutch friction material and causes judder.

## How it is serviced

Fluid changed with the brake fluid. Replaced with the clutch.
