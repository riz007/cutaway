A second gearbox bolted behind the main one on a four-wheel-drive vehicle, splitting drive between the rear axle and a propshaft running forward to the <Part id="drivetrain.differential_front" />.

## What it does

Two things. It engages and disengages front drive, and it offers a low range.

On a part-time system there is no centre differential, so front and rear are locked at the same speed. That is excellent on loose ground and unusable on grip: as the vehicle turns, front and rear axles travel different distances, and with no differential to absorb the difference the driveline winds up and either judders or breaks something. This is why part-time four-wheel drive must not be used on dry sealed roads.

Low range is a second reduction, usually around 2.5:1, multiplying torque and dividing speed. It is not for more power but for control: it lets the engine idle the vehicle over an obstacle or down a descent with fine throttle resolution and strong engine braking.

## How it fails

Actuator and shift-fork faults leaving it stuck in one mode, chain stretch on chain-driven cases, and seal leaks.

## How it is serviced

Oil on schedule. Engaging four-wheel drive occasionally keeps the mechanism free.
