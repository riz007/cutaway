A moulded plastic vessel, usually saddle-shaped so it can straddle the propshaft or exhaust, mounted ahead of the rear axle where the structure protects it best in a rear impact.

## What it does

It stores fuel, but it also has to manage vapour and slosh. Internal baffles stop fuel surging away from the pump pickup under braking and cornering — a nearly empty tank on a long fast bend is where a car will cut out. A vent path carries vapour to the <Part id="fuel.evap_canister" /> rather than to atmosphere.

Multi-layer plastic construction replaced steel because it can be moulded around obstructions, cannot rust, and deforms rather than splitting in a crash. The layers include a barrier film specifically to stop hydrocarbons permeating through the plastic.

<Market region="Thailand">
With E20 gasohol now the mainstream petrol grade, tank and line materials must be ethanol-compatible. Vehicles built in Thailand from 2008 onward are; much older vehicles and aftermarket tanks may not be, and ethanol will attack unsuitable rubber and plastic.
</Market>

## How it fails

Physical damage from road debris, and split filler or vent hoses. Leaks are a fire risk.

## How it is serviced

Not serviced. Water and sediment are dealt with by the <Part id="fuel.fuel_filter" />.
