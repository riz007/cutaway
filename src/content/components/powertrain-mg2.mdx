The larger electric machine, geared to the ring gear and so directly to the wheels. It is what actually drives a hybrid most of the time in traffic.

## What it does

As a motor it provides torque from zero rpm, which covers exactly the region a combustion engine is worst at — pulling away and low-speed acceleration. That is why a hybrid feels brisk in town and why its engine can stay off there entirely.

As a generator it recovers energy. Lift off or brake and the wheels drive MG2 backwards through the gearset; it becomes a generator, the <Part id="electrical.inverter" /> rectifies the output into the pack, and the resistance the driver feels is energy being banked rather than thrown away as heat. <Part id="braking.regen_blending" /> decides how much of the deceleration it provides.

Because it is tied to the ring gear, its speed is proportional to road speed at all times.

<Warning>
High voltage. Its windings can generate a dangerous voltage whenever the wheels turn, even with the vehicle switched off and the pack isolated.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Seldom. Coolant ingress and resolver faults are the documented modes.

## How it is serviced

Transaxle fluid and, on liquid-cooled units, coolant, both on schedule.
