A friction disc squeezed between the <Part id="powertrain.flywheel" /> and a spring-loaded pressure plate. It exists because an engine cannot idle at zero road speed while connected to stationary wheels.

## What it does

It transmits torque by friction, and its whole value lies in being able to slip in a controlled way. Pulling away is a deliberate, managed slip: the engine turns at idle or above, the wheels start at zero, and the clutch absorbs the difference as heat until the two speeds meet.

Clamp load comes from a diaphragm spring, so the clutch is engaged by default and the pedal works by pushing against that spring to release it. That failure direction is deliberate — a broken release mechanism leaves the drive connected rather than stranding the car with no drive at all.

The disc carries torsional damper springs around its hub to absorb engine firing pulses that would otherwise rattle the gearbox.

## How it fails

By wearing out, accelerated by riding the pedal or slipping it excessively on hills. A worn clutch slips under load: engine speed rises without a matching increase in road speed, most obvious in a high gear at full throttle. Oil contamination from a leaking crankshaft or gearbox seal causes judder and premature failure.

## How it is serviced

Not adjustable on hydraulic systems. Replaced as an assembly with the release bearing.
