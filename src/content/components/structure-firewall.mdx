The panel separating the engine bay from the cabin, and one of the most heavily loaded and heavily penetrated panels in the vehicle.

## What it does

It is a barrier in three senses. Against fire, as the name says, it is a steel panel between the occupants and everything flammable. Against noise and heat, it carries thick sound-deadening and insulation on both faces, and it is the single largest contributor to how quiet a cabin is. Against intrusion, it is a structural member that resists the engine being pushed backwards in a frontal impact.

It is also densely penetrated: steering column, pedal linkage, wiring harnesses, heater hoses, air conditioning pipes, brake lines and the throttle all pass through it. Every one of those needs a sealed grommet, and every grommet is a potential path for water, noise and fumes.

It carries the <Part id="interior.pedal_box" /> and much of the HVAC case, so it must be stiff enough that pedal effort does not flex it.

## How it fails

Water ingress through failed grommets, which is a common cause of wet footwells and mysterious electrical faults, and corrosion in the lower corners where the scuttle drains.

## How it is serviced

Not serviced. Grommets and drains should be checked when damp carpets appear.
