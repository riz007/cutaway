A thermostat is a valve operated by a pellet of wax. It is one of the simplest parts in the car and one of the most consequential, because it decides when the engine is allowed to lose heat.

## What it does

Cold, an engine is inefficient, wears faster and produces more emissions. So on start-up the thermostat stays shut and coolant circulates only through the block and the <Part id="cooling.bypass_hose" />, warming a small volume of fluid quickly.

As coolant temperature rises the wax melts and expands, pushing a pin that cracks the valve open. Flow begins to divert through the <Part id="cooling.radiator" />. A typical unit starts to open around 82 degrees Celsius and is fully open near 94. Between those points it modulates, holding the engine at its designed operating temperature across a wide range of loads.

<Note>
Watch the coolant temperature in the cooling scenario. It climbs quickly, then the rate flattens sharply as the thermostat cracks open. That inflection is the thermostat doing its entire job.
</Note>

## How it fails

Two ways, with very different consequences. Stuck open means the engine never warms properly: weak cabin heat, poor economy, and a temperature gauge that sits low. Stuck shut means coolant cannot reach the radiator at all, and the engine overheats within minutes even though the radiator itself is stone cold.

## How it is serviced

Replaced when faulty, and often replaced as a matter of course whenever the cooling system is opened for other work.
