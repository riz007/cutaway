A tube with a coarse mesh strainer on the end, hanging just above the bottom of the <Part id="lubrication.sump" />. It is where the whole lubrication circuit begins.

## What it does

It gives the <Part id="lubrication.oil_pump" /> a supply. Its height is a careful compromise: too high and it draws air when the oil level drops or surges; too low and it picks up the sludge and debris that settle on the sump floor.

The mesh is deliberately coarse — far coarser than the <Part id="lubrication.oil_filter" /> — because its job is only to stop objects large enough to jam the pump. A fine screen here would restrict flow and could starve the pump, which is far more dangerous than letting small particles through to the filter that exists to catch them.

Because it is on the suction side, it is the one part of the circuit where blockage is silent. A restricted pickup does not raise pressure at the gauge; it lowers it, and by then the bearings are already short of oil.

## How it fails

Blockage by sludge, which is the classic consequence of long-neglected oil changes. It is one of the main mechanisms by which a missed service history eventually destroys an engine.

## How it is serviced

Not accessible without dropping the sump. Protected entirely by changing oil on schedule.
