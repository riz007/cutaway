A crumple box is a short section of the front rail engineered to fold rather than resist. Its whole value lies in failing predictably.

## What it does

Injury in a crash comes from deceleration, and deceleration is the change in speed divided by the time taken. The speed change is fixed by the collision. The only variable a designer controls is time — and time comes from distance.

The crumple box folds like a concertina at a designed force, converting kinetic energy into plastic deformation over a few hundred millimetres. Those millimetres are what turn an instantaneous stop into a decelerating one. Beading and holes pressed into the walls act as folding initiators so the collapse begins where the engineer intended.

Behind it, the <Part id="structure.front_rail" /> and the passenger cell are deliberately much stiffer. Soft in front, rigid around the occupants, and the <Part id="safety_adas.airbag_front" /> filling the gap in between.

<Note>
The crumple box is also why low-speed impacts are repairable. It is designed to be bolted, so a parking knock replaces a bolt-in part rather than requiring the rail itself to be pulled straight.
</Note>

## How it fails

Only by doing its job. Once deformed it has no residual energy absorption and must be replaced, not straightened.

## How it is serviced

Never serviced. After any frontal impact its condition, and that of the crash beam ahead of it, is part of the structural assessment.
