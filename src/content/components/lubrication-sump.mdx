The pan bolted under the engine that holds the oil when it is not in circulation, and the lowest point of the whole assembly.

## What it does

It is a reservoir, and it is sized generously for a reason: oil needs time to settle. Oil returning from the bearings and the head is full of entrained air, and foamy oil cannot be pumped or support a bearing film. Sitting in the sump lets those bubbles rise out before the <Part id="lubrication.pickup" /> draws the oil round again.

It is also a cooler. On many engines the sump is the primary route by which heat leaves the oil, which is why some are finned or made of aluminium rather than pressed steel.

Internal baffles and a windage tray stop oil surging away from the pickup under braking and cornering, and shield the sump from the crankshaft, which would otherwise whip the surface into foam.

## How it fails

Impact damage from road debris — it is the lowest and most exposed part of the engine — stripped drain plug threads, and gasket leaks.

Deep standing water during the wet season is a real hazard. Beyond the risk of hydraulic lock through the intake, a sudden dunk in cold floodwater can crack a hot sump.

## How it is serviced

Drained at each oil change. Its plug washer should be renewed each time.
