A heat exchanger that keeps oil temperature in its working range, usually an oil-to-coolant plate stack sandwiched at the filter housing.

## What it does

Oil has a narrow useful temperature band. Too cold and it is thick, so it flows poorly at start-up and does not reach the far bearings quickly; it also fails to boil off the water and fuel that condense into it, which is how sludge and acids form. Too hot and its viscosity collapses, the film thins, and the base oil begins to oxidise and break down.

Coupling the oil to the coolant circuit handles both ends. When the engine is cold, coolant warms faster than oil and the exchanger actually heats the oil, shortening warm-up. When the engine is working hard, the flow reverses and coolant carries heat away. It is more accurately a temperature stabiliser than a cooler.

Hard-working vehicles — towing, or a turbocharged engine — may add a separate oil-to-air radiator.

Sustained heat, heavy loads and slow traffic all push oil temperature up while limiting the airflow available to reject heat, so the oil cooler works harder here than the same vehicle would in a temperate market.
</Market>

## How it fails

Internal cracking that lets oil and coolant mix, visible as emulsion in either fluid.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item; its seals are renewed when the filter housing is disturbed.
