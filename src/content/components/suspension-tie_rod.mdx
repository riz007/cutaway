The link from each end of the <Part id="suspension.steering_rack" /> to the steering arm on the hub. It is the last mechanical connection between the driver's hands and the direction of the wheel.

## What it does

It pushes and pulls the hub to steer the wheel. It has a ball joint at each end: an inner one at the rack, protected by a rubber bellows, and an outer one at the hub, protected by its own boot. Both are needed because the wheel steers and moves vertically at the same time.

Its length is adjustable by a threaded section, and that adjustment sets the front toe — whether the wheels point slightly towards or away from each other. Toe is the alignment setting tyres are most sensitive to; a small error scrubs tread away quickly and shows as feathered wear across the tread blocks.

The rod's length is also chosen so that its arc matches the suspension's arc through travel. Getting this wrong produces bump steer, where the wheel steers itself as it moves over a bump — which is why lifting or lowering a vehicle without correcting the geometry can make it wander.

## How it fails

Outer joint wear giving play and uneven tyre wear; torn boots and bellows admitting water.

## How it is serviced

Play checked at every service. Alignment set after any replacement.
