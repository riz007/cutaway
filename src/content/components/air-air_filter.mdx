A pleated paper or synthetic element that every cubic metre of air entering the engine must pass through — and an engine at speed swallows a startling amount, well over a thousand litres a minute.

## What it does

It removes airborne grit. Silica dust is harder than the cylinder bore, and any that reaches the combustion chamber goes to work on the bore, the <Part id="powertrain.piston_rings" /> and then, via the oil, on the bearings. Filtration is the cheapest wear protection in the vehicle.

Pleating gives a large surface area in a small frame, which keeps the pressure drop low. That matters because any restriction is a pumping loss the engine has to pay for.

Counter-intuitively, a filter gets more efficient as it loads: the trapped dust layer becomes part of the filter medium. It is replaced not because it stops filtering but because the pressure drop eventually costs power and economy.

<Market region="Thailand">
Dusty dry-season conditions and heavy urban particulate loading shorten filter life considerably. On upcountry and unsealed-road use, inspect at half the book interval rather than waiting for it.
</Market>

## How it fails

By clogging. Symptoms are sluggishness and worse economy long before any warning light.

## How it is serviced

Replaced on interval. Never washed if it is a paper element.
