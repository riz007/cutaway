The same differential function as the front, housed in its own casing at the rear axle, and additionally responsible for turning the drive through ninety degrees.

## What it does

Torque arrives along the vehicle from the <Part id="drivetrain.propshaft" /> and must leave across it to the wheels. A hypoid crown wheel and pinion do both jobs at once: they redirect the drive and provide the final reduction, typically around 3.5:1.

The hypoid geometry — with the pinion offset below the crown wheel centre — is deliberate. It lowers the propshaft and so lowers the transmission tunnel, and it puts more teeth in contact at once, which is quieter and stronger. The cost is a great deal of sliding rather than rolling between the teeth, which is why a hypoid axle needs a specific extreme-pressure oil and will destroy itself on ordinary gear oil.

Limited-slip and locking differentials add clutches or a dog engagement to send torque to the wheel with grip.

## How it fails

Pinion bearing wear giving a whine that changes between drive and overrun, and pinion seal leaks.

## How it is serviced

Oil on schedule, of the exact specification. Axles that are regularly submerged should have the oil checked for water.
