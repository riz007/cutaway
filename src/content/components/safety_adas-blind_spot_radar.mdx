A short-range radar in each rear corner of the vehicle, watching the adjacent lanes alongside and behind.

## What it does

It covers the region the mirrors cannot. Even a well-adjusted mirror set leaves a wedge beside and slightly behind the car where a vehicle is invisible, and it is exactly where an overtaking car sits during a lane change.

Detection is not enough on its own; it has to distinguish a vehicle in the next lane from the crash barrier, a parked line of cars, or a vehicle two lanes over. It does that by tracking relative velocity and lateral position over time, so a stationary roadside object is filtered out while a car closing at a few kilometres per hour is not.

The same sensors provide rear cross-traffic alert when reversing out of a parking space, where they can see approaching traffic long before the driver can.

They are mounted inside the plastic bumper, which is transparent to radar — but metallic paint, stickers and towbar accessories are not necessarily so.

<Market region="Thailand">
Motorcycles filtering between lanes are the common case here and the hardest for these systems: they are small radar targets, approach fast and appear from unexpected angles. The alert is a supplement to a shoulder check, not a replacement for one.
</Market>

## How it fails

Misalignment after a rear impact, and obstruction from bumper repairs or accessories.

## How it is serviced

Calibration after rear bumper work.
