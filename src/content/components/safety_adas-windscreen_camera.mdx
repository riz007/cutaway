A camera, often a stereo pair, mounted high on the inside of the windscreen behind the mirror, looking through the swept area of the glass.

## What it does

It recognises things. Radar knows where an object is; the camera knows what it is — a lane marking, a speed limit sign, a pedestrian, a cyclist, the tail of a truck. That classification is what enables lane keeping, traffic sign recognition, automatic high beam and pedestrian detection.

Its position is not arbitrary. It needs the highest possible viewpoint for the longest view down the road, and it must sit within the wiper's swept area so rain does not blind it.

Everything depends on knowing exactly where it is aimed. The system converts pixel positions into real-world distances and angles, and that conversion assumes a precisely known mounting geometry. A camera off by a fraction of a degree misjudges where the lane edge is at a hundred metres.

<Warning>
After a windscreen replacement the camera must be recalibrated to the manufacturer's procedure. A car driven away without calibration may have lane keeping and emergency braking that are subtly and dangerously wrong rather than obviously absent.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Obstruction by dirt, stickers or a chip in its field of view; and loss of calibration.

## How it is serviced

Its area of glass kept clean. Recalibrated after screen replacement, alignment work or suspension height changes.
