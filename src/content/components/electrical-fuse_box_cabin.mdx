The interior fuse panel, usually behind a trim cover in the footwell or the end of the dashboard, handling the lower-current circuits.

## What it does

It distributes and protects everything that lives inside: the instrument cluster, infotainment, interior lighting, window and mirror motors, the diagnostic socket, and the permanent feeds that keep memory alive while the car sleeps.

Being inside means a far kinder environment than the engine bay — no heat cycling, no water, no vibration — which is why the delicate low-current circuits live here.

It is also where the parasitic drain investigation starts. A modern car draws a small current permanently to keep modules alive, and pulling fuses one at a time while measuring that current is the standard way to find a circuit that is not going to sleep.

<Note>
Many cabin boxes carry a printed legend on the inside of the cover mapping each fuse to its circuit, along with spare fuses and a puller.
</Note>

## How it fails

Rarely, and usually only after a spillage. A drink emptied into a footwell box causes corrosion and a set of unrelated-looking electrical faults.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Repeated failure of the same fuse means a fault in that circuit, never a reason to fit a larger fuse.
