The load deck behind the cab of a pickup, and the reason the vehicle exists.

## What it does

It carries the payload and spreads it into the <Part id="structure.ladder_frame" /> beneath through a set of body mounts. Corrugations pressed along its length stiffen a thin sheet enough to take a tonne without permanent deformation, and to resist the point loading of a pallet corner or a heavy tool.

Being a bolted-on structure rather than a welded one is what makes the platform so versatile: the same chassis cab takes a standard bed, a flatbed, a tipper or a service body.

The bed is deliberately isolated from the frame on rubber mounts, so frame flex on uneven ground does not distort the bed and so load noise does not transmit into the cab.

Drain holes at the corners let rainwater out, since the bed is open to the weather by definition.

## How it fails

Corrosion, which starts wherever the coating has been scraped through by cargo, and around the drain holes and mount points. Dents from point loading.

<Market region="Thailand">
Working pickups carry loads that scrape the coating quickly, and the wet season does the rest. A spray-in or drop-in bed liner is genuinely protective rather than cosmetic here.
</Market>

## How it is serviced

Drains kept clear; damaged coating touched in before it rusts.
