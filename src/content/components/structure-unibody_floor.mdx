The stamped and spot-welded floor pan with its longitudinal and transverse members. On a monocoque vehicle it is the primary structure — there is no separate chassis underneath.

## What it does

It carries every load: the suspension mounting points, the seat and belt anchorages, the powertrain via the <Part id="suspension.subframe_front" />, and the torsional stiffness that determines how the whole car feels.

Torsional rigidity is the number that matters. A body that twists lets the suspension geometry change under load, so the dampers are working against a moving reference and the car never settles. Stiffness is what allows suspension tuning to mean anything.

The floor achieves it through shape rather than thickness. Pressed ribs, closed box sections formed by welding two stampings together, and cross-members turn thin sheet into a rigid structure at low weight.

Different grades of steel are used deliberately in different places: ultra-high-strength steel around the passenger cell to resist intrusion, and softer grades in the crush zones that are meant to fold.

## How it fails

Corrosion, particularly in box sections that trap water, and accident damage that pulls the structure out of alignment.
