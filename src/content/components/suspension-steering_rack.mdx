A pinion on the end of the steering column meshing with a straight-toothed rack that slides side to side, pushing one <Part id="suspension.tie_rod" /> and pulling the other.

## What it does

It converts rotation into linear movement, with a ratio chosen to balance effort against response. A quick rack needs fewer turns lock to lock and makes the car feel alert, but demands more effort and transmits more kickback; a slower rack is calmer and more relaxing. Variable-ratio racks vary the tooth pitch along their length, so steering is relaxed around the straight-ahead and quickens as lock is applied.

Rack and pinion replaced the older recirculating ball box on almost everything because it is direct: fewer joints between the wheel and the road means less lost motion and better feel.

The rack is spring-loaded against the pinion by an adjustable yoke, which takes up backlash. Too loose gives a knock; too tight gives stiff, notchy steering that does not self-centre.

Assistance is added by the <Part id="suspension.eps_motor" /> acting on the rack or the column.

## How it fails

Worn rack bushes and inner ball joints, giving a knock over bumps and play at the wheel; and, on hydraulic systems, leaking seals.

## How it is serviced

Inspected for play and for torn bellows, which let water into the inner joints.
