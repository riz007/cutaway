A pivoting lever in the <Part id="interior.pedal_box" />, and the first multiplication in the braking chain.

## What it does

It multiplies force by leverage. A typical ratio of around 4:1 turns 150 newtons of foot pressure into 600 at the pushrod, before the <Part id="braking.booster" /> multiplies it again.

Its travel is as important as its force. Pedal feel — the relationship between how far your foot moves and how hard the car slows — is deliberately engineered, because drivers modulate braking by feel rather than by force. A pedal that goes soft or long tells you something is wrong long before performance is actually compromised, which is why a change in feel is a symptom worth taking seriously.

The pedal box is designed to collapse or release in a heavy frontal impact so the pedal is not driven back into the driver's foot.

## How it fails

Worn pivot bushes give lost motion and a sloppy feel. A pedal that slowly sinks under steady pressure indicates internal leakage in the <Part id="braking.master_cylinder" />. A pedal that is hard and high suggests lost assistance.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Pedal feel is assessed at every brake inspection.
