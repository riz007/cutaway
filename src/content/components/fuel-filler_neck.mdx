The tube from the flap to the tank, plus a surprising amount of safety engineering for something that looks like a pipe.

## What it does

It gets fuel into the tank and vapour back out, and it prevents misfuelling by geometry. A diesel nozzle is physically larger than a petrol one and will not enter a petrol filler; many petrol cars add a spring-loaded flap that only the correct nozzle opens. It is a mechanical interlock, and it is the reason misfuelling is far more common in the diesel-into-petrol-car direction than the reverse.

The neck also creates the fill-up shut-off. A small vent tube senses when rising fuel blocks it, which changes the pressure at the nozzle tip and trips its automatic cut-off. Continuing to squeeze past that point is what forces liquid fuel into the <Part id="fuel.evap_canister" />.

Capless systems replace the screw cap with a sealed spring-loaded door in the neck itself.

## How it fails

Corrosion at the tank end on steel necks, and perished rubber joining hoses that weep fuel and leak vapour.

## How it is serviced

Inspected. The cap's seal is worth checking when an evaporative-leak fault appears.
