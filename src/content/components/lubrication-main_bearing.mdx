A pair of thin steel-backed shells lined with soft bearing metal, clamped around each crankshaft journal. There is no roller or ball anywhere in it.

## What it does

It supports the crankshaft on a film of oil. In normal running the journal never touches the shell: rotation drags oil into the converging gap and generates hydrodynamic pressure high enough to lift the shaft clear. The bearing is, in effect, floating on a wedge of oil a few micrometres thick.

The lining is deliberately soft — aluminium-tin or a copper-lead alloy. That seems wrong for a load-bearing surface until you consider what it buys: any hard particle that gets through the filter embeds itself into the soft lining rather than scoring the hardened crank journal, which is far more expensive to replace. The bearing is designed to be the sacrificial part.

That soft layer also allows a small amount of conformability, so a slightly out-of-true journal beds in rather than concentrating load at one edge.

## How it fails

Oil starvation, which collapses the film and lets metal meet metal within seconds; and contamination beyond what the lining can embed. The symptom is a deep knock that worsens under load.

## How it is serviced

Never, in service. Clean oil at pressure is the entire maintenance regime.
