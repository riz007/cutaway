An air-conditioning circuit with valving that lets it run in either direction, so it can heat the cabin as well as cool it.

## What it does

A combustion car heats its cabin with waste heat it has no use for. An electric car has almost none, so the obvious answer is a resistive heater — and resistive heating is honest but expensive, converting a kilowatt of battery energy into exactly a kilowatt of heat, straight out of the range.

A heat pump does better by moving heat rather than creating it. Reversing the circuit makes the outside exchanger the evaporator and the inside one the condenser, so it collects heat from ambient air and pumps it inside. Because it is moving existing heat, a kilowatt of electrical input can deliver two or three kilowatts of heating.

The same hardware also lets the vehicle harvest waste heat from the motor, inverter and pack, which is heat that would otherwise be dumped overboard.

<Market region="Thailand">
Cabin heating is nearly irrelevant in this climate, so a heat pump's headline range benefit largely does not apply. Its value here is on the cooling and battery-conditioning side, where the same efficient circuit reduces the air-conditioning load that does hurt range.
</Market>

## How it fails

Reversing valve faults, leaving the system stuck in one mode.

## How it is serviced

As part of normal air-conditioning service.
