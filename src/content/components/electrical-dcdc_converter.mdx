An electronic converter that steps the traction pack's several hundred volts down to the ~14 V the ordinary vehicle systems expect. On an electrified vehicle it does the alternator's job.

## What it does

An electrified car still has an entirely conventional 12 V network — lights, wipers, ECUs, infotainment, the <Part id="braking.abs_modulator" />. Rather than carry a second charging system, the DC-DC converter takes the energy it needs from the <Part id="powertrain.hv_battery" />.

It does this by switching the high voltage into a small transformer at tens of kilohertz, then rectifying and smoothing the output. High frequency is what lets the transformer be small; a mains-frequency transformer of the same rating would be enormous.

It is better than an alternator in two respects: it is more efficient, and it works with the engine off, which is precisely the condition a hybrid spends much of its time in.

<Warning>
High voltage on its input side. Only trained personnel with the correct equipment should open one.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Thermal failure of the power stage, and coolant leaks on liquid-cooled units. The symptom is a 12 V battery that goes flat despite the traction pack being charged.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Its cooling circuit is maintained with the rest of the thermal system.
