The head closes the top of each cylinder and carries almost everything that controls breathing: the valves, their seats and guides, the camshafts above them, the injectors or spark plugs, and the ports that air travels through.

## What it does

Its shape defines the combustion chamber, and that shape decides how the charge tumbles and burns. A well-designed chamber burns quickly and evenly, which allows more compression before knock sets in — and compression ratio is one of the biggest levers on efficiency an engine has.

The head also carries its own coolant passages, cast to sit close to the exhaust valve seats and the spark plug boss, because those are the hottest points in the engine.

Almost all modern heads are aluminium: lighter than iron and roughly three times better at conducting heat away, at the cost of expanding more when hot.

## How it fails

Warping from a single serious overheat is the common one, and it takes the <Part id="powertrain.head_gasket" /> seal with it. Cracks between valve seats appear on high-mileage or badly overheated engines. Valve seat recession is now rare on unleaded-designed engines.

## How it is serviced

Nothing routine. When removed, the deck is checked for flatness with a straight edge and feeler gauge and skimmed if it is outside tolerance.
