A transformer that turns the vehicle's 12 volts into the tens of thousands needed to jump the <Part id="powertrain.spark_plug" /> gap.

## What it does

It stores energy in a magnetic field, then collapses that field suddenly. Current through the primary winding builds a field in the iron core; when the ECU switches the primary off, the field collapses across the secondary winding, which has far more turns. The turns ratio multiplies the voltage — a ratio of a hundred to one turns a few hundred volts of collapse into tens of kilovolts.

The voltage produced is only ever as high as it needs to be. The spark jumps as soon as the gap breaks down, which clamps the voltage there. A worn plug with a wide gap forces the coil to work harder, which is why worn plugs kill coils.

Dwell time — how long the primary is energised before firing — determines how much energy is stored, and the ECU adjusts it with engine speed and battery voltage.

Coil-on-plug designs put one coil directly on each plug, removing HT leads entirely.

## How it fails

Insulation breakdown from heat and age, which lets the spark track to earth instead of jumping the gap. The symptom is a misfire under load, when cylinder pressure makes the gap hardest to bridge.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Replace worn plugs on interval to protect them.
