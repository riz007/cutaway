A spherical bearing: a hardened ball stud in a socket, letting the hub swivel for steering while also pivoting as the suspension moves.

## What it does

It provides rotation in every direction at once. That is unavoidable at the outer end of the <Part id="suspension.control_arm_lower" />, because the wheel must steer left and right through a wide arc while simultaneously travelling up and down.

It is loaded far more heavily than its size suggests. The lower joint on a strut suspension carries a share of the vehicle's weight, the cornering load, and the shock of every impact, in a bearing perhaps 30 mm across.

Everything depends on the rubber boot retaining grease and excluding water and grit. There is no route by which a joint survives a torn boot for long.

<Warning>
A separated ball joint lets the wheel fold under the car and causes immediate loss of control. Play in a ball joint is not a defer-it item.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Wear opening up play, felt as a knock over bumps and vague steering. Almost always preceded by a split boot.

<Market region="Thailand">
Water intrusion during flooding, and grit from unsealed roads, both dramatically shorten joint life once the boot is compromised.
</Market>

## How it is serviced

Checked for play at every service by rocking the wheel. Boots inspected at the same time.
