Three thin rings in grooves near the top of the <Part id="powertrain.piston" />, each with a different job, together making the difference between an engine that runs and one that does not.

## What they do

The top compression ring seals combustion gas. It works by being pushed outward by the very pressure it is sealing: gas gets behind the ring and forces it against the bore, so the harder the cylinder fires, the harder the ring seals.

The second ring catches what the first misses and helps scrape oil down.

The oil control ring is the clever one. It meters a film of oil onto the bore just thick enough to separate ring from iron, then scrapes the excess back through slots into the crankcase. Too much and the engine burns oil; too little and the rings wear.

## How they fail

Wear opens the end gaps and lets combustion gas past into the crankcase as blow-by, which the <Part id="air.pcv_valve" /> then has to handle. Carbon can stick a ring in its groove so it can no longer expand — common on direct-injection engines and on engines run on short journeys.

## How they are serviced

Not serviceable. Their condition is inferred from a compression or leak-down test.
