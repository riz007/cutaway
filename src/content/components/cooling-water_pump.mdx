The water pump is a simple centrifugal impeller that keeps coolant moving. Without flow, the coolant in the hottest passages boils while the coolant in the radiator sits cold.

## What it does

An impeller spins inside a volute, throwing coolant outward and drawing more in behind it. The output feeds the block, through the water jacket, up into the head, and on to the <Part id="cooling.thermostat" />, which decides whether it goes to the radiator or straight back round the bypass.

Most pumps are belt or chain driven from the crankshaft, so flow scales with engine speed — convenient, since heat output does too. Electric pumps are increasingly common because they can be run independently of engine speed, and can keep running after shutdown to pull heat out of a hot <Part id="powertrain.turbocharger" />.

## How it fails

The shaft seal is the weak point. When it goes, coolant seeps out of a weep hole beneath the pump, which is deliberate: it is designed to leak visibly rather than let coolant into the bearing. Bearing failure follows and produces a rumble that changes with engine speed. On a chain-driven pump, seizure can take the timing drive with it.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item as such, but on engines where the pump is driven by the timing belt it is replaced with the belt as a matter of routine, because the labour is already being spent.
