A long fabric bag folded into the roof rail above the doors, deploying downward to cover the side glass for both rows of seats.

## What it does

It protects heads. In a side impact there is very little distance between an occupant's head and the door glass or the striking vehicle, and the head is both heavy and fragile. The curtain covers the entire glass area so the head is cushioned wherever it goes.

It behaves differently from a front airbag in one important respect: it stays inflated for several seconds rather than deflating immediately. That is because it also has to work in a rollover, which unfolds over a much longer period than a collision, and it must keep occupants inside the vehicle for the duration — ejection through a side window is one of the most lethal outcomes in a rollover.

That is also why it is triggered by rollover sensing as well as by side impact.

<Warning>
Pyrotechnic. The inflator and the folded bag run the length of the roof rail, directly behind the headlining. Never fit accessories, grab handles or roof-mounted items into that area without checking the deployment path.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Squib and connector faults, reported by the airbag warning lamp.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Handled as an explosive article when removed.
