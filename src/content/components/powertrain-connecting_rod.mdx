The rod turns the piston's straight-line push into rotation at the crank throw. It is one of the most highly stressed parts in the engine and one of the simplest in shape.

## What it does

On the power stroke it is in compression, carrying the full force on the piston crown. But near the top of the exhaust stroke it is in tension, because it has to stop the piston and drag it back down — and at high rpm that inertial load can exceed the combustion load. This is why over-revving an engine breaks rods even with the throttle shut.

Its I-beam section is the lightest shape that resists buckling under that compression. The big end runs on a plain bearing fed from the <Part id="lubrication.oil_gallery" /> through a drilling in the <Part id="powertrain.crankshaft" />.

<Note>
The rod's length relative to the crank throw is why piston motion is not a simple sine wave, and why the strokes are not symmetric. A shorter rod means more side thrust against the bore and more wear.
</Note>

## How it fails

Big-end bearing failure from oil starvation, then seizure and breakage. A broken rod usually exits through the block.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Clean oil at pressure is the whole story.
