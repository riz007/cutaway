The oil pump creates the pressure that every plain bearing in the engine depends on. It is not there to move oil around for its own sake; it is there to maintain a pressurised film that keeps metal surfaces apart.

## What it does

Driven from the crankshaft, the pump draws oil from the sump through the <Part id="lubrication.pickup" />, pushes it through the <Part id="lubrication.oil_filter" />, and out into the <Part id="lubrication.oil_gallery" />. From there it is fed to the main and big-end bearings, up to the camshafts, and to any component that needs pressurised oil — including the <Part id="powertrain.vvt_actuator" /> and the <Part id="powertrain.turbocharger" />.

A relief valve caps the pressure, so a cold, thick oil does not burst a filter or a gallery plug.

<Note>
Pressure is not flow, and the gauge measures the resistance to flow rather than the amount. That is why oil pressure falls as the engine warms and the oil thins, and why worn bearings show up as low pressure — the clearances are letting oil escape faster than the pump can supply it.
</Note>

## How it fails

Wear in the gears or rotor lowers output, usually alongside general engine wear. A blocked pickup strainer, from sludge in a neglected engine, starves the pump entirely. Both end the same way: bearing damage.

## How it is serviced

Not directly. It is protected by changing the oil and filter on schedule with the correct grade.
