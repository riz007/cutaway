A poppet valve: a stem with a tapered head that seals against a matching seat in the <Part id="powertrain.cylinder_head" />. It opens inward, into the cylinder, and is closed by a spring.

## What it does

It admits the charge, and its size is the single biggest restriction on how much air the engine can breathe. That is the whole reason for four-valve heads — two smaller intake valves give far more open area than one large one, and are lighter to accelerate.

It runs comparatively cool, because incoming air washes over it on every cycle. That cooling is also why intake valves stay cleaner on port-injected engines, where fuel sprays across the back of the valve and continually washes it.

## How it fails

On direct-injection engines the fuel no longer touches the valve, so oil vapour from the <Part id="air.pcv_valve" /> bakes onto the back of it as hard carbon. Over tens of thousands of kilometres that deposit chokes the port and causes misfires and rough idle. It is a design consequence, not a fault.

## How it is serviced

Valve clearance is checked at the manufacturer's interval on engines with mechanical adjusters. Carbon build-up is removed by walnut blasting or chemical cleaning.
