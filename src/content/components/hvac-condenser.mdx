A flat heat exchanger mounted in front of the <Part id="cooling.radiator" />, where refrigerant gives up the heat collected from inside the car.

## What it does

Hot high-pressure vapour arrives from the <Part id="hvac.compressor" />. Air passing through the fins carries heat away, and as the refrigerant loses heat it condenses to a liquid — which is where the component gets its name and where most of the heat transfer happens, because a phase change moves far more energy than simply cooling a gas.

The heat rejected here is the sum of the heat taken out of the cabin plus the work the compressor put in, so a condenser always has more to shed than the evaporator absorbed.

Its position ahead of the radiator is a compromise: it needs the coldest available air, but it then warms the air reaching the engine radiator, which is why the <Part id="cooling.cooling_fan" /> runs whenever the air conditioning is on.

<Market region="Thailand">
Condenser performance sets the ceiling on cooling capacity, and it is limited by ambient temperature. At 38 degrees, a partly blocked core that would go unnoticed in a temperate climate produces obvious loss of cooling in traffic. Fin blockage by dust and insects is a leading cause of poor air conditioning here.
</Market>

## How it fails

Stone damage and corrosion causing leaks, and blocked fins.

## How it is serviced

Rinsed clear of debris. Not a replacement item.
