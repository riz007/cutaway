A separate tank of urea solution — 32.5 percent urea in demineralised water — dosed into the exhaust to deal with the one pollutant a diesel is worst at.

## What it does

Diesels burn lean and hot, and hot lean combustion makes oxides of nitrogen. A three-way catalyst cannot reduce NOx in an oxygen-rich exhaust, so a different chemistry is needed.

Selective catalytic reduction supplies it. Urea injected into the hot exhaust decomposes to ammonia, and over the SCR catalyst the ammonia reacts with NOx to produce plain nitrogen and water. It is genuinely effective — around a 60 percent reduction in NOx over a comparable engine without it.

Consumption runs at roughly two to five percent of fuel used, so the tank needs topping up between services rather than at them.

<Market region="Thailand">
SCR arrived in volume with the Euro 5 diesel standard from January 2024, so AdBlue is a new maintenance item for many Thai owners. The vehicle warns well in advance, then legally must refuse to restart once the tank is empty — that is emissions regulation, not a fault.
</Market>

## How it fails

Crystallised deposits at the injector, and a frozen or contaminated tank. Never top up with anything but the correct specification fluid.

## How it is serviced

Topped up by the owner or at service.
