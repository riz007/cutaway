A bench or split bench, and structurally more demanding than it looks, because it sits directly in front of the luggage area.

## What it does

It carries occupants and their restraints — belt anchorages and, importantly, the ISOFIX and top tether points for child seats. Those anchorages bolt into the body structure, not the seat foam, and their strength is regulated: a child seat in a crash applies very large loads through small mounting points.

Its backrest is also a barrier. In a frontal impact, unsecured luggage in the boot arrives at the seat back with enormous force, and the latch that holds a folding backrest upright is what stops it entering the cabin. That is why a folding rear seat's latch is a safety item and why loose heavy objects in a boot are genuinely dangerous.

Folding mechanisms have to reconcile that strength requirement with the convenience of a one-touch release, so the latch is usually a robust hook engaging a striker welded to the body.

## How it fails

Latch mechanisms that no longer lock securely, and worn folding hinges.

## How it is serviced

Latches checked for positive engagement. ISOFIX anchorage covers should be present and the points free of debris.
