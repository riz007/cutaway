A small heat exchanger that cools recirculated exhaust gas with engine coolant before it re-enters the intake.

## What it does

Recirculated exhaust reduces NOx by lowering peak combustion temperature. But exhaust gas leaves the manifold at several hundred degrees, and feeding it hot into the intake partly defeats the purpose — it raises intake charge temperature and displaces more air than necessary.

Cooling it first means a given mass of recirculated gas absorbs more heat during combustion and displaces less oxygen, so NOx falls further for less penalty in power and soot. Diesel engines, which need large EGR fractions to meet emissions limits, depend on it.

The cost is that it puts exhaust gas and coolant either side of a thin wall, at very different temperatures, cycling constantly. It is a demanding thermal fatigue application.

## How it fails

Two ways. Soot fouling on the gas side reduces its effectiveness and eventually blocks it. More seriously, internal cracking lets coolant into the exhaust or intake stream, which shows as unexplained coolant loss, white smoke and, in bad cases, coolant reaching a cylinder.

## How it is serviced

Not a routine item. Suspect it when a diesel loses coolant with no external leak.
