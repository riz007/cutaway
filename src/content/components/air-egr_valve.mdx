A valve that deliberately routes a measured amount of spent exhaust gas back into the intake. It sounds like sabotage and is in fact careful chemistry.

## What it does

Oxides of nitrogen form when combustion gets hot enough — above roughly 1,600 degrees Celsius — for nitrogen and oxygen in the air to combine. The way to prevent that is to keep peak temperature down.

Recirculated exhaust is inert: it has already burnt, so it takes no part in combustion, but it does absorb heat. Adding it to the charge lowers the peak flame temperature and cuts NOx sharply, with no fuel penalty. On a throttled petrol engine it also reduces pumping loss, because the cylinder is partly filled with exhaust and the throttle can open further for the same power.

On diesels the flow is large enough to need the <Part id="cooling.egr_cooler" /> in the loop.

## How it fails

Soot. Exhaust gas carries carbon, and the valve and its passages coke up over time until the valve sticks — open, giving a rough idle and poor low-speed running, or closed, raising NOx and often triggering a fault.

## How it is serviced

Not routine, but a known cleaning job on high-mileage diesels, particularly those used mostly for short urban trips.
