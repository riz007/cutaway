The central screen and its computer, which on most modern vehicles has absorbed navigation, media, phone, climate control and vehicle settings.

## What it does

It is a networked control surface rather than a radio. It sits on the <Part id="electrical.can_bus" /> and sends requests to other modules — asking the climate module for a temperature change, or the <Part id="electrical.ecu_body" /> to alter a lighting preference — rather than driving anything directly.

That consolidation is driven by cost and flexibility: one screen replaces dozens of switches and can be updated in software. The ergonomic cost is real and increasingly acknowledged, because a physical switch can be found by touch while driving and a flat glass menu cannot. This is why climate temperature, volume and hazard lights are returning to physical controls on many vehicles.

It usually holds the microphone, amplifier and connectivity hardware, and it is a route into the vehicle network, which makes it a security boundary as much as a convenience feature.

## How it fails

Software faults resolved by a reset, screen delamination and touch layer failure, and heat damage.

<Market region="Thailand">
Sustained heat in a parked car is hard on displays and their adhesive layers. Screen delamination and bubbling are more common here than in temperate markets, and shade or a sunshade meaningfully extends life.
</Market>

## How it is serviced

Software updates. Not otherwise serviceable.
