A welded steel or cast aluminium frame bolted beneath the front of the body, carrying the engine mounts, the control arms and the steering rack.

## What it does

It concentrates the suspension and powertrain loads into a stiff local structure and delivers them into the body at a handful of well-reinforced points, rather than spreading many separate attachments across relatively thin sheet metal.

It also lets the whole front end be assembled as a module and bolted in from below, which matters enormously for manufacturing cost and for major repair.

Most importantly for refinement, it is usually isolated from the body on rubber bushes. That gives a second stage of isolation: road noise and vibration must pass through the suspension bushes into the subframe, then through the subframe bushes into the body. Two stages of rubber are far more effective than one, and this is a large part of why a modern car is quiet.

Its precise position also sets the front alignment, since the control arm pivots are attached to it.

## How it fails

Corrosion, particularly around the mounting points, and perished isolation bushes giving a clunk and vague steering.

## How it is serviced

Inspected for corrosion. Alignment must be checked if it is ever removed.
