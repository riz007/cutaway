Rigid steel tube, usually double-walled and coated, running from the <Part id="braking.abs_modulator" /> along the floor to each corner. It makes up most of the length of the hydraulic circuit.

## What it does

It carries pressure without expanding. That is its entire specification, and it is why the line is rigid rather than flexible: any expansion under pressure is fluid that moves without applying the brakes, felt at the pedal as sponginess. A brake line that swells is a brake line that has stolen pedal travel.

Routing follows the body structure, clipped at short intervals so it cannot vibrate or chafe, and kept away from heat and from anything that moves.

Fittings are flared rather than sealed with washers, so the seal is metal on metal and does not rely on a perishable component.

<Market region="Thailand">
Underbody corrosion is the main enemy, and coastal humidity plus flood-water immersion during the wet season accelerate it sharply. A vehicle that has been driven through standing salt or brackish water deserves an underbody inspection, since a line usually corrodes from the outside in.
</Market>

## How it fails

External corrosion, then a pinhole under pressure. Impact damage from road debris is the other cause.

## How it is serviced

Inspected visually. Sections are replaced rather than patched.
