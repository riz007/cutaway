The same idea as the intake camshaft, working against a much harder problem: its valves open into gas that may still be at several hundred degrees and well above atmospheric pressure.

## What it does

It opens the exhaust valve before the piston reaches bottom dead centre, deliberately giving up a little expansion work. The reason is blowdown — letting the remaining pressure escape on its own means the piston does not have to push against it on the way up, and the pumping loss saved is worth more than the expansion given away.

Near the top of the exhaust stroke both valves are briefly open together. That overlap lets outgoing exhaust help draw the fresh charge in, which is free scavenging at the right rpm and a rough idle at the wrong one.

On a turbocharged engine, exhaust timing also governs how much energy reaches the <Part id="powertrain.turbocharger" />.

## How it fails

Lobe and journal wear, as with the intake, plus more heat soak through the valve stems.

## How it is serviced

Nothing routine beyond oil condition.
