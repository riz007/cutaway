A sealed box near the battery holding the vehicle's high-current fuses and its main relays. It is the first branch point of the entire electrical system.

## What it does

Every circuit needs protection sized to its wiring, not to its load. A fuse exists to protect the cable, so that a short circuit burns a cheap replaceable link rather than setting the harness on fire. High-current circuits — the <Part id="cooling.cooling_fan" />, glow plugs, the ABS pump — are fused here because this is where the supply enters.

Relays live here too. A relay lets a small switch control a large current: the switch on the dashboard carries a fraction of an amp to the relay coil, and the relay's contacts carry the twenty or more amps the load actually needs. That keeps heavy cable runs short and out of the cabin.

Its position in the engine bay is a compromise between short cable runs and a hostile environment of heat, vibration and water.

## How it fails

Water ingress through a poorly seated lid causing corrosion, and relay contacts that burn and develop resistance — which shows as a load that works intermittently or weakly.

## How it is serviced

Not routine. Always replace a fuse with the same rating; a higher one removes the protection the cable was designed around.
