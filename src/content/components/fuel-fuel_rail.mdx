A tube bolted across the engine with the injectors plugged into it. Simple in appearance, and doing something more subtle than just distributing fuel.

## What it does

It holds every injector at the same pressure. That matters because injector delivery is calculated from pulse width alone, and pulse width only translates to a known mass of fuel if pressure is known and equal at each nozzle.

It also acts as an accumulator. Each injector opening is a sudden demand that would otherwise drop pressure locally and send a pulse back down the line. The rail's internal volume damps those pulses so cylinder four gets the same fuelling as cylinder one.

On a direct-injection engine the rail is a far heavier forging, because it holds 200 bar or more rather than the 3 to 5 bar of a port-injected system. Diesel common rails run higher still, fed by the <Part id="fuel.hp_pump" />.

<Warning>
Pressurised, and on direct-injection and diesel systems dangerously so. A high-pressure spray can penetrate skin. Never slacken a rail fitting on a running or recently run engine.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Leaks at the injector seals rather than in the rail itself.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item.
