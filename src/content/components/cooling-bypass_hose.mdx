A small hose, often barely visible, that lets coolant circulate around the engine while the <Part id="cooling.thermostat" /> is still closed.

## What it does

It solves a problem created by the thermostat. With the thermostat shut, the radiator circuit is blocked, but the <Part id="cooling.water_pump" /> keeps turning. Without somewhere for the flow to go, the pump would churn against a dead head and, worse, coolant sitting still in the <Part id="cooling.water_jacket" /> would form hot spots around the exhaust valve seats while the thermostat, further away, stayed cool and refused to open.

The bypass gives that flow a short circuit back to the pump inlet. Coolant keeps moving through the block and head, so heat distributes evenly, the thermostat sees a representative temperature, and the whole engine warms uniformly and quickly.

<Note>
This is why a cold engine warms up fast: only a small volume of coolant is in circulation. Once the thermostat opens, the much larger radiator volume joins the loop and the rate of temperature rise flattens noticeably.
</Note>

## How it fails

Perishing and leaking. Because it is small and often hidden under the intake, a bypass leak can be hard to locate.

## How it is serviced

Replaced with the thermostat when the system is opened.
