A manual plug, usually bright orange, that physically breaks the high-voltage battery into two halves. It exists for exactly one reason: so a person can make the pack safe with their own hands and see that they have done it.

## What it does

The pack is a long series string. Removing the disconnect opens that string near its midpoint, so neither remaining half is at a lethal potential, and no current can flow through the pack at all.

It is a mechanical interlock, not a software one. That distinction is the whole point — the <Part id="electrical.contactor" /> is opened by the <Part id="electrical.bms" />, which is a computer, and a computer can fail or be commanded wrongly. A plug in a technician's pocket cannot.

Most designs include a small interlock loop that signals its removal, so the vehicle knows to shut down cleanly, and a delay before the plug can be fully withdrawn so the contactors open under no load.

<Warning>
Removing the disconnect does not make the system safe on its own. Capacitors in the <Part id="electrical.inverter" /> hold charge for several minutes, and the correct procedure is always to isolate, wait, and then prove the circuit dead with an instrument before touching anything. This app is a teaching aid, not a service manual, and high-voltage work requires specific training and protective equipment.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Corrosion of its contacts and damage to the interlock circuit, both of which will keep the vehicle from entering ready mode.

## How it is serviced

Only as part of a documented high-voltage isolation procedure.
