The large box near the tailpipe that takes broadband exhaust noise down to something legal and liveable.

## What it does

Where a resonator targets particular frequencies, the muffler attacks the whole spectrum, usually with two mechanisms.

Reflective sections route gas through perforated tubes and baffled chambers so sound waves are repeatedly turned back on themselves and cancel. Absorptive sections pack the space around a perforated tube with mineral wool or steel fibre, which converts sound energy into a small amount of heat.

Every one of those obstacles costs back pressure, and back pressure costs power, so a muffler is a continuous negotiation between noise and performance. That is the honest engineering behind aftermarket exhausts: most are louder mainly because they impose less restriction, and how much power that returns on a standard engine is usually far less than the noise increase suggests.

## How it fails

Rot from the inside out. Short trips are the cause: the exhaust never gets hot enough to drive off the water that combustion produces, so condensate sits in the lowest box and corrodes it. The muffler is the part that fails first for exactly this reason.

## How it is serviced

Inspected for corrosion and secure mountings.
