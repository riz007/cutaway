An electric pump living inside the tank, submerged in the fuel it is pumping. It sits in a swirl pot with a coarse strainer and delivers fuel up to the engine at pressure.

## What it does

It supplies more fuel than the engine can use and the surplus returns to the tank, or on returnless systems the pressure is regulated at the pump. Either way the point is a steady supply pressure at the <Part id="fuel.fuel_rail" />, because injector metering depends on it.

Mounting it inside the tank looks odd until you consider the alternatives. A pump pushes liquid far better than it pulls it, so being at the bottom of the supply avoids cavitation. And the surrounding fuel both cools the motor and, since there is no oxygen in a sealed tank, removes any risk from the brushes sparking.

<Note>
This is why running consistently near empty shortens pump life: the pump loses its coolant and runs hot.
</Note>

## How it fails

Wear from contaminated fuel, and heat damage from chronic low-fuel running. Symptoms are hard hot starting, misfire under load, then failure to start.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. It is protected by clean fuel and a maintained filter.
