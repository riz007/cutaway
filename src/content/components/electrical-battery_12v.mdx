Every vehicle has one, including a battery-electric one. Its job is to be a large, short-term current source and to hold the low-voltage network up when nothing else is generating.

## What it does

Three tasks. It supplies the enormous, brief current the <Part id="electrical.starter_motor" /> needs — often over 150 amps. It powers the accessories when the engine is off. And it stabilises the network voltage, absorbing spikes and filling in dips that the <Part id="electrical.alternator" /> cannot respond to fast enough.

Capacity is quoted two ways for two different jobs: amp-hours describe how long it can supply a small load, and cold cranking amps describe how much current it can deliver briefly at low temperature. They are not interchangeable.

<Note>
In an electric car the 12 volt battery is what wakes the vehicle up. It powers the computers that close the <Part id="electrical.contactor" /> to connect the traction pack. A flat 12 volt battery immobilises an EV with a full traction battery, which surprises people.
</Note>

## How it fails

Sulphation from being left partly discharged, plate shedding from vibration, and internal shorts. Symptoms are slow cranking, then unexplained electronic faults as voltage sags below what the modules expect.

## How it is serviced

Terminals cleaned, hold-down checked, and state of health tested at service. Replacements on modern vehicles usually have to be registered with the charging system so it applies the right charge profile.
