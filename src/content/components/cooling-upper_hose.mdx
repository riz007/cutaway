The large moulded rubber hose carrying hot coolant from the thermostat housing to the top of the <Part id="cooling.radiator" />.

## What it does

It is the hot side of the main loop, and it is flexible for a reason: the engine sits on rubber mounts and moves relative to the body-mounted radiator, so a rigid connection would fatigue and crack.

It is moulded to shape rather than cut from straight stock, so it follows its route without kinking and without touching anything hot or sharp.

It also has to survive a pressurised system. At about one bar above atmospheric and up to 120 degrees Celsius, the reinforcing fabric plies inside the rubber are what stop it ballooning.

<Note>
The hose is a useful diagnostic. Squeeze it when the engine is warm — it should be firm with pressure. A cold upper hose on a hot engine points at a <Part id="cooling.thermostat" /> that has not opened.
</Note>

<Warning>
Under pressure and full of coolant well above its atmospheric boiling point when hot. Never disconnect a hose on a warm engine.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Age hardening and cracking, softening and swelling from oil contamination, and chafing where it has been left touching a bracket.

## How it is serviced

Inspected by squeezing when cold. Replaced when hard, spongy or crazed.
