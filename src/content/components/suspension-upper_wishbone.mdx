The upper link of a double-wishbone front suspension, found on pickups and body-on-frame SUVs where load capacity and geometry control matter more than packaging.

## What it does

Together with the <Part id="suspension.control_arm_lower" /> it forms a four-bar linkage that controls exactly how the wheel moves through its travel. Because the designer can choose the length, height and angle of both arms independently, they can dictate camber change, roll centre height and scrub radius almost at will.

That is the decisive advantage over a strut. Under body roll, a well-designed double wishbone keeps the outer wheel closer to upright, so more of the tyre stays flat on the road exactly when it is most loaded. Typically the upper arm is made shorter than the lower, which produces the desired negative camber gain in bump.

It also lowers the top of the suspension, which is why it suits vehicles that need a low bonnet line or a lot of steering lock.

The cost is more parts, more joints and more space.

## How it fails

Ball joint and bush wear, giving a knock and camber that drifts out of specification.

## How it is serviced

Inspected for play; some designs have a greaseable joint.
