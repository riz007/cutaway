A torsion bar wound into a helix. When the spring compresses, the wire is not bending — it is twisting along its length.

## What it does

It carries the vehicle's static weight and determines how far the wheel moves for a given force. Its rate depends on wire diameter, coil diameter and the number of active coils, which gives designers a lot of freedom in a small package.

Choosing that rate is the central ride-and-handling compromise. Soft springs give a comfortable ride and large body movements; stiff springs control the body and transmit more of the road. Because the spring only decides how far the wheel moves — not how quickly it stops — it is meaningless without the damper inside <Part id="chassis_hydraulics.damper_internals" />.

Progressive springs vary the pitch along their length so the closely wound coils touch and go inactive under load, giving a soft initial rate and a firmer one as the suspension compresses. That is how a vehicle can ride well empty and still cope loaded.

## How it fails

Corrosion pitting that becomes a fatigue crack, then a snapped coil — usually announced by a clunk and a corner sitting low. Springs also sag slowly with age.

Inspected. Replaced in axle pairs.
