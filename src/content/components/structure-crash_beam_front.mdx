A steel or aluminium beam spanning the full width of the nose, mounted to the front rails through the crumple boxes. It is the first structural thing an obstacle meets.

## What it does

It distributes. An impact rarely arrives conveniently in line with a rail; it might be offset, or against a narrow object. The beam spreads that load across both rails so the whole front structure participates rather than one side taking everything.

It also standardises engagement height, so two vehicles in a collision meet structure to structure instead of one sliding underneath the other.

For low-speed impacts it is designed to be the sacrificial part, which is why insurance crash tests are conducted at walking-pace speeds: a well-designed beam and crush can absorb a car park impact entirely, leaving only bolt-on parts to replace.

Many also carry a lower stiffener that catches a pedestrian's legs below the knee, rotating them onto the bonnet rather than under the car.

## How it fails

Deformation in impact, and corrosion where the plastic bumper cover has trapped moisture against it.

## How it is serviced

Not serviced. Inspected behind the bumper cover after any frontal impact, however minor the cover damage appears.
