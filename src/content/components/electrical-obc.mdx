The converter that lets an electric vehicle charge from ordinary AC mains. It is the reason AC charging is limited to a handful of kilowatts while DC charging is not.

## What it does

Batteries take direct current; the grid supplies alternating current. Something has to rectify and regulate, and on AC charging that something rides in the car.

It draws AC, rectifies it, corrects the power factor so the vehicle behaves as a well-mannered load, and produces a controlled DC output at whatever voltage the pack is currently at, under the supervision of the <Part id="electrical.bms" />.

Its rating sets AC charging speed — typically 7 to 11 kW — and that limit is about weight, cost and cooling, not about the pack. This is exactly why DC rapid charging is different in kind rather than degree: a DC charger does the conversion in the roadside cabinet, where size does not matter, and feeds the pack directly through the <Part id="electrical.charge_port" />, bypassing the on-board charger entirely.

<Warning>
High voltage on both its input and output sides.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Thermal failure of the power stage, usually after prolonged charging in high ambient temperature.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Its cooling circuit is maintained with the rest of the thermal system.
