Accelerometers — one set inside the airbag control module, plus satellite sensors in the front structure and the door sills — whose job is to make an irreversible decision in a few thousandths of a second.

## What it does

It distinguishes a real crash from everything else. That sounds trivial and is the hardest part of the whole restraint system: a kerb strike, a pothole, a slammed door and a heavy stone can all produce a sharp acceleration spike, and deploying airbags for any of them would be both dangerous and expensive.

The module therefore does not act on peak acceleration. It integrates the signal over time to derive the velocity change, and compares that against calibrated thresholds. A pothole gives a big spike but almost no sustained velocity change; a genuine impact gives a smaller peak sustained for far longer.

Satellite sensors at the front and in the sills provide early confirmation and tell the module where the impact came from, so it can choose which devices to fire — front airbags, curtains, pretensioners, or the fuel pump cut-off — and at what stage.

The whole decision, from first contact to firing, takes 15 to 30 milliseconds.

## How it fails

Connector corrosion and sensor faults, which disable the restraint system and light the airbag lamp.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Sensors in a deformed area are replaced after a crash.
