A sensor threaded into the main gallery, reporting the pressure the <Part id="lubrication.oil_pump" /> is maintaining. On many vehicles it is a simple switch rather than a transducer.

## What it does

It watches the one parameter that, if lost, destroys the engine in under a minute. That is why the oil pressure warning is red and unfiltered — unlike the coolant gauge, which is heavily damped to avoid alarming drivers, the oil lamp is a threshold and it means stop now.

The distinction between pressure and flow is worth understanding. The lamp monitors pressure, which is really a measure of resistance to flow. Worn bearings with excessive clearance let oil escape faster than the pump can supply, so pressure falls — the gauge is indirectly reporting bearing condition.

Pressure also falls naturally as oil warms and thins, and rises with engine speed. A reading that is low at hot idle but recovers with revs is the classic early sign of bearing wear.

## How it fails

Sensor and wiring faults give false warnings. A false alarm and a real one look identical from the driver's seat, so both must be treated as real until the pressure is confirmed with a mechanical gauge.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item.
