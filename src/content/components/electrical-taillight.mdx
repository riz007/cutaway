One housing carrying several functions that must remain visually distinct: position, brake, indicator, reverse and rear fog.

## What it does

It communicates intent. The brake light exists to tell the driver behind that you are slowing, and its brightness relative to the position lamp is regulated so the change is unmistakable in daylight yet not blinding at night — the same filament or LED array is often used for both at two different intensities.

The indicator must be amber and must be separated enough from the brake light to remain readable when both are active. Reverse lamps are white, both to signal reversing and to light the ground behind.

LEDs brought a real safety gain beyond styling: they reach full brightness in microseconds rather than the couple of hundred milliseconds a filament needs to heat up. At motorway speed that difference is several metres of stopping distance for the car behind.

The high-level third brake light sits in the driver's forward line of sight to the car in front.

## How it fails

Moisture ingress causing corrosion and condensation, and individual LED segment failure, which usually condemns the whole unit.

## How it is serviced

Checked at every service. A failed bulb is a roadworthiness item.
