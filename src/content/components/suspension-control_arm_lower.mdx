The link between the subframe and the bottom of the hub carrier. On a MacPherson strut car it is the single most important locating member.

## What it does

It controls where the wheel is. The <Part id="suspension.front_strut" /> above handles vertical load and provides the upper pivot; the control arm sets the wheel's lateral position and resists everything trying to push it out of place — cornering forces, braking drag, and the impact of a pothole.

Its geometry sets the front roll centre and much of the camber change through suspension travel, which is why an arm that is bent even slightly ruins the alignment in ways that cannot be adjusted out.

It is attached with two rubber bushes at the inboard end and a <Part id="suspension.ball_joint" /> outboard. The bushes are deliberately not equally stiff: the rear one is usually softer and often fluid-filled, which lets the wheel move backwards a few millimetres over a sharp bump. That compliance is a large part of what makes a car ride well over a bad surface.

## How it fails

Perished or split bushes, giving vague steering, a knock over bumps and uneven tyre wear. Corrosion on pressed-steel arms.

## How it is serviced

Inspected for play. Alignment checked after replacement.
