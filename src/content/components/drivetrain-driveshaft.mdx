The shaft from the differential to each driven wheel, with a <Part id="drivetrain.cv_joint" /> at each end.

## What it does

It transmits torque to a wheel that is simultaneously steering, moving up and down through the suspension travel, and changing its distance from the differential as it does so. The outer joint handles the steering angle, the inner joint plunges to absorb the length change.

On a transverse front-drive car the two shafts are usually different lengths, because the differential sits off-centre in the transaxle. That asymmetry causes torque steer — the two sides twist by different amounts under power and the car pulls to one side. Some cars fix it with an intermediate bearing and a stub shaft, making both driveshafts equal length.

Many shafts carry a damper weight to suppress a resonance that would otherwise cause boom at one particular speed.

## How it fails

Almost always through a split rubber boot, which lets grease out and grit in. Left alone, the joint wears rapidly. Shafts themselves rarely break outside abuse.

<Market region="Thailand">
Flood water and mud accelerate boot degradation, and a boot that has been submerged should be inspected even if it looks intact — water inside the joint is as damaging as grit.
</Market>

## How it is serviced

Boots inspected at every service. A boot replaced promptly is a fraction of the cost of a shaft.
