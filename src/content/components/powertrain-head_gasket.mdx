A thin layered steel shim doing three sealing jobs at once, each against a different pressure and a different fluid.

## What it does

Around each bore it must hold back combustion pressure — fifty atmospheres or more, thousands of times a minute. Around the coolant passages it seals about one bar of hot coolant. Around the oil drainbacks it seals oil at gallery pressure. And it does all three across a joint where an aluminium <Part id="powertrain.cylinder_head" /> and a different metal below it expand by different amounts every time the engine warms up.

Modern gaskets are multi-layer steel: several thin embossed sheets that act as springs, so the seal stays live as the joint moves. Head bolt torque is what preloads them, which is why bolts are tightened in sequence and often to an angle rather than a torque.

## How it fails

Three signatures worth knowing. Combustion into the coolant pressurises the system and pushes bubbles into the expansion tank. Coolant into a cylinder gives white steam and a sweet exhaust smell. Coolant into the oil emulsifies it to a pale sludge on the filler cap.

## How it is serviced

Never as maintenance. Overheating is what kills it, so the real service is keeping the cooling system honest.
