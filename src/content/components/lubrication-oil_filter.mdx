Engine oil picks up combustion soot, metallic wear particles and dirt. The filter takes out what it can before that debris reaches a bearing clearance measured in tens of microns.

## What it does

All the oil the pump delivers passes through a pleated medium that traps particles down to around twenty microns. The pleating gives a large surface area in a small can, which matters because the filter has to flow the pump's full output at cold-start viscosity without an excessive pressure drop.

Two valves are built in. An anti-drainback valve keeps the filter full when the engine is off, so pressure builds immediately at start-up rather than after the filter refills. A bypass valve opens if the medium blocks or the oil is too thick to pass, on the principle that unfiltered oil is far better than no oil.

## How it fails

By being left in too long. A saturated filter runs permanently on its bypass valve, at which point the engine is circulating unfiltered oil and nobody is aware of it. Poor-quality filters can also shed medium or have weak anti-drainback valves, which shows up as a rattle for a second or two on cold start.

## How it is serviced

Replaced with every oil change, without exception. It costs a fraction of the oil and protects everything the oil touches.
