Not a part so much as a void: the cast cavities inside the block and head through which coolant flows around the cylinders and combustion chambers.

## What it does

It gets coolant to where heat actually is. The distribution is deliberately uneven — passages are cast narrower and closer to the exhaust valve seats and the spark plug boss, which are the hottest points, and more generous around the cooler intake side.

Flow control matters as much as volume. Drilled restrictors in the head gasket meter how much coolant reaches each cylinder, so the rearmost cylinder is not starved while the front one is over-cooled. Getting this wrong produces an engine that reliably fails one particular cylinder.

Modern engines also split the circuit, running the head cooler than the block: a hotter block reduces friction, a cooler head reduces knock.

## How it fails

Internal corrosion and scale when coolant is left beyond its interval or topped up with plain water, which narrows the passages and insulates the walls. Electrolytic corrosion attacks aluminium specifically when the coolant's inhibitors are exhausted.

Topping up with tap water is a common habit and a genuinely damaging one. Beyond removing corrosion inhibitors, dissolved minerals deposit as scale exactly at the hottest points, where the insulation does most harm.

## How it is serviced

Only by changing coolant on schedule with the specified type.
