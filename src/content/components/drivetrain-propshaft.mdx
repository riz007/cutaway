A tube carrying torque the length of the vehicle from the gearbox to the rear axle. Simple, and constrained by some interesting physics.

## What it does

It transmits torque while accommodating movement at both ends. The gearbox is bolted rigidly to the body; the rear axle moves up and down and fore and aft on its suspension. Universal joints at each end absorb the angle, and a splined slip joint absorbs the length change.

It is a tube rather than a bar for stiffness: in torsion, material near the outside does nearly all the work, so a hollow tube gives almost the same strength as a solid bar at a fraction of the weight.

Length is limited by whirl. Every shaft has a critical speed at which it becomes unstable and bows, and that speed falls as the shaft gets longer. Long-wheelbase vehicles therefore use a two-piece shaft with a centre bearing, which halves the free length and pushes the critical speed safely above anything encountered in use.

## How it fails

Worn universal joints giving a clunk on take-up, a failed centre bearing giving vibration at a particular speed, and lost balance weights.

## How it is serviced

Joints greased at service where nipples are fitted; otherwise inspected for play.
