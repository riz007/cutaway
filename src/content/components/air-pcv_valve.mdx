A one-way metering valve that vents the crankcase back into the intake. A small part solving a problem that would otherwise wreck the engine.

## What it does

No piston ring seals perfectly. A fraction of combustion gas always escapes past the <Part id="powertrain.piston_rings" /> into the crankcase — blow-by. Left alone it would pressurise the crankcase, blow out the seals, and contaminate the oil with fuel, water and acids.

Positive crankcase ventilation draws those gases out using manifold vacuum and burns them in the engine. The valve meters that flow: at idle, manifold vacuum is high and the valve restricts, so it does not upset the mixture; under load, vacuum is low and the valve opens wider to handle the greater blow-by.

<Note>
This is also the source of the oil mist that coats intake tracts, and the reason direct-injection engines suffer intake valve carbon — there is no longer a fuel spray washing the valve clean.
</Note>

## How it fails

Sticking. Stuck closed pressurises the crankcase and pushes oil out of the seals and dipstick tube. Stuck open causes a large vacuum leak and a lean, rough idle.

## How it is serviced

Some manufacturers specify replacement on interval. Worth checking when oil leaks appear at several seals at once.
