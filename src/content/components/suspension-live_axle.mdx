A single rigid housing containing the <Part id="drivetrain.differential_rear" /> and both half shafts, with a wheel bolted to each end. The whole assembly moves as one.

## What it does

It drives and locates both rear wheels with one component. Because the housing is rigid, the two wheels are always parallel and camber never changes — the tyres stay flat on the road regardless of body roll, which is genuinely useful on a loaded vehicle.

It is also extremely strong and simple, tolerant of heavy loads and rough ground, and cheap to repair. Those are exactly the priorities for a pickup or a body-on-frame SUV.

The drawback is unsprung mass. The differential and shafts move with the wheels rather than being carried by the body, so there is a lot of weight for the dampers to control, and the ride over sharp bumps suffers. The wheels are also not independent: a bump on one side tilts the whole axle and disturbs the other.

Coil-sprung versions with locating links ride considerably better than leaf-sprung ones while keeping the strength.

## How it fails

Pinion and wheel bearing wear, axle seal leaks contaminating the brakes, and bent housings after heavy impact.

## How it is serviced

Oil on schedule; breather kept clear, since a blocked breather forces oil out past the seals.
