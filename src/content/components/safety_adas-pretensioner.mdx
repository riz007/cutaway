A pyrotechnic device in the belt reel or buckle that pulls the belt tight in the first milliseconds of a crash, before the occupant has begun to move.

## What it does

A seat belt always has slack: webbing that is not quite flat against the body, plus a small amount of reel spool and clothing compression. That slack has to be taken up before the belt starts restraining, and during those few centimetres the occupant is accelerating freely.

The pretensioner removes it. A small gas generator drives a piston or spins the reel, retracting several centimetres of webbing in about 10 milliseconds — before the <Part id="safety_adas.airbag_front" /> even begins to inflate. The occupant is coupled to the decelerating car from the very start.

Its partner is the load limiter, which does the opposite: once the crash forces exceed a set level, a torsion bar in the reel deliberately yields and pays webbing back out. Restraining too hard would break ribs, so the belt is tightened first, then allowed to give in a controlled way — with the airbag catching what the belt lets go.

<Warning>
Pyrotechnic. Contains an explosive charge and must never be probed, powered or heated.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Squib circuit faults, flagged by the airbag lamp.

## How it is serviced

Not serviceable. Replaced after deployment, never reused.
