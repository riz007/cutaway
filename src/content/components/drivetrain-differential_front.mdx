The gearset that lets the two driven front wheels turn at different speeds while both receive drive. On a transverse car it lives inside the <Part id="drivetrain.transaxle" />.

## What it does

Through a corner the outer wheel travels further than the inner one, so they must rotate at different speeds. Lock them together and one has to scrub, which wastes energy, wears tyres and makes the steering fight back.

An open differential solves this with a beautifully simple arrangement: the crown wheel drives a carrier, and inside the carrier small pinion gears mesh with a side gear on each output shaft. Travelling straight, nothing rotates within the carrier and both wheels turn together. In a corner, the pinions rotate on their pins, letting one output speed up by exactly as much as the other slows.

Its weakness follows from the same mechanism: it always applies equal torque to both sides, so if one wheel is on ice it can only deliver what that wheel can hold — which is nearly nothing. Traction control fixes this by braking the spinning wheel, which raises the torque the differential can transmit to the other.

## How it fails

Bearing and pinion wear from neglected oil, and cracked housings after driveshaft failure.

## How it is serviced

Oil on schedule.
