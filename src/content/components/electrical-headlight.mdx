Not a lamp in a housing but a precision optical instrument, shaped to put light exactly where it is useful and nowhere it is not.

## What it does

The hard requirement is the cut-off. A dipped beam must light the road well ahead while stopping sharply below the eye level of oncoming drivers, and asymmetrically — throwing further on the near side to light the verge and signs without dazzling anyone. That shape is produced by a reflector's computed surface, or by a projector's shield edge imaged through a lens.

LED sources changed the packaging but not the requirement. Their small size allows thin housings and multi-element matrix designs that can selectively black out the section of beam containing another vehicle, keeping high beam everywhere else.

Automatic levelling compensates for a loaded boot tipping the beam upward.

<Market region="Thailand">
Poorly aimed and over-bright aftermarket conversions are widespread and genuinely dangerous. Dropping an LED bulb into a housing designed for a halogen filament puts the light source in the wrong place for the reflector, destroying the cut-off and scattering glare, even though it looks brighter to the owner.
</Market>

## How it fails

Lens clouding from UV, moisture ingress through failed seals, and driver module failure on LED units.

## How it is serviced

Aim checked periodically and after any front-end repair.
