A brutally simple, very high current DC motor whose only job is to turn the engine fast enough to start itself.

## What it does

It has to spin the engine to roughly 200 rpm against compression, and it needs a lot of torque to do it — which means a lot of current, often over 150 amperes and briefly far more. That is why it has its own heavy cable straight from the <Part id="electrical.battery_12v" /> rather than going through a fuse box.

A solenoid on top does two things at once when energised: it pushes a pinion gear forward to engage the ring gear on the <Part id="powertrain.flywheel" />, and only then closes a heavy contact that feeds the motor. The order matters — engaging a spinning pinion would destroy both gears.

A one-way clutch in the pinion lets it disengage the instant the engine fires and starts turning faster than the motor.

Stop-start systems use a reinforced starter designed for far more cycles.

## How it fails

Worn brushes, burnt solenoid contacts giving the familiar single click, and a worn pinion or ring gear producing a grinding noise.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Slow cranking is usually the battery or its cables, not the starter.
