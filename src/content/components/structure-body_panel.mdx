The outer skin: bonnet, doors, wings, roof and tailgate. On a monocoque, some of these are structural and some are not, and knowing which is which matters.

## What it does

The roof and the fixed wings are welded in and contribute to body stiffness. Bolt-on panels — bonnet, doors, boot lid, and on many cars the front wings — are essentially cladding, shaped for airflow, pedestrian impact and appearance.

Panel shape is not only styling. A large flat panel drums and vibrates, so curvature is added to stiffen it — this is why almost no body panel is truly flat. Aerodynamic form matters too: drag is a major share of energy use at motorway speed, and on an EV it directly determines range.

Pedestrian protection sets the bonnet's height and the clearance beneath it, so there is deformation space above the hard parts of the engine.

Materials are mixed deliberately: steel where strength and repairability matter, aluminium on bonnets to reduce weight at the front, and plastic composites on bumper covers and some wings because they resist minor impacts elastically.

## How it fails

Corrosion starting at stone chips and panel edges, and impact damage.
