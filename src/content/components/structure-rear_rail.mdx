The longitudinal member running back from the floor to the rear bumper, and the rear crash structure.

## What it does

It carries the rear suspension loads and the boot floor, and in a rear impact it collapses in the same controlled way as the front rail — with one extra constraint that shapes its design entirely.

The <Part id="fuel.tank" /> or the traction pack sits inside the wheelbase, ahead of the rails, and must not be reached. So the rear structure is designed to absorb the impact energy behind the tank and then stop, with a stiff transition where the rails meet the floor. Rear crash testing is largely about proving fuel system integrity.

The rear crush zone is generally shorter than the front, because closing speeds in rear impacts are usually lower and because there is no engine to package around.

On an EV the rails also protect the pack, which extends nearly to the rear axle, leaving even less crush distance and demanding a stiffer, more carefully staged structure.

## How it fails

Corrosion, and impact damage. Rear rail deformation frequently accompanies apparently minor boot damage.

## How it is serviced

Not serviced. Measured after any rear impact, particularly on vehicles with a traction pack.
