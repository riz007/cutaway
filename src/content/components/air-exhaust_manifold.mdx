The first component in the exhaust, collecting gas from each port into a single outlet — and the part that lives with the harshest thermal cycling in the vehicle.

## What it does

It carries exhaust away, and its geometry matters for the same wave reasons as the intake. Each exhaust pulse leaving a cylinder creates a low-pressure wave behind it; time that wave to arrive at another cylinder's port during valve overlap and it helps scavenge burnt gas out. Equal-length tubular headers exist to control that timing precisely.

On a turbocharged engine the manifold is instead designed to deliver exhaust energy to the turbine as hot and as fast as possible, so runners are short and often cast integrally with the <Part id="powertrain.turbocharger" /> housing or even the <Part id="powertrain.cylinder_head" />.

It goes from ambient to several hundred degrees within a minute of starting, and back, thousands of times.

<Warning>
Among the hottest surfaces on the vehicle. It can glow visibly under load and stays dangerous long after shutdown.
</Warning>

## How it fails

Cracking from thermal fatigue, and studs that shear or corrode. The symptom is a tapping noise on cold start that quietens as the metal expands and the crack closes.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item.
