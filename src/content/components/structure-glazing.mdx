The windscreen, side windows and rear screen — and they are deliberately made of two different kinds of glass, for two different safety reasons.

## What it does

The windscreen is laminated: two sheets of glass bonded to a plastic interlayer. When it breaks it cracks but stays in one piece, held together by the interlayer. That matters because the windscreen is a structural member — it is bonded rather than sealed in, contributing to body stiffness and to keeping the roof up in a rollover — and because it stops occupants being ejected and blocks most ultraviolet.

Side windows are toughened instead: heat-treated so they shatter into small blunt granules rather than shards. That is chosen so occupants are not cut by broken glass, and so the glass can be broken out for escape or rescue. It is also why a laminated side window, fitted on some cars for security and quietness, cannot be broken in an emergency the same way.

Modern windscreens also carry the mounting and calibrated field of view for the <Part id="safety_adas.windscreen_camera" />, plus heating elements, aerials and acoustic interlayers.

## How it fails

Stone chips that spread into cracks, especially with thermal shock from air conditioning on a hot screen.

## How it is serviced

Chips repaired early. After a windscreen replacement, the camera must be recalibrated or the driver-assistance systems will be aiming incorrectly.
