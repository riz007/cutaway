A high-strength steel beam inside each door, running diagonally or horizontally between the hinge and latch ends.

## What it does

Side impacts are the hardest to protect against, because there is almost no distance between the outside of the car and the occupant — perhaps 200 millimetres, against a metre or more at the front. There is no room for a crumple zone, so the strategy is different: rather than absorb energy by deforming, the structure must resist intrusion and move the occupant out of the way.

The bar's job is the resisting part. It spans the door opening and transfers the impact load into the <Part id="structure.a_pillar" /> and <Part id="structure.b_pillar" /> and thence into the floor and roof, so the load is carried by the whole body rather than by a thin door skin.

It works with the <Part id="safety_adas.airbag_curtain" /> and the seat-mounted side airbag, which fill the small remaining gap. Structure resists, airbags cushion.

## How it fails

Only in an impact. It is made of very high-strength steel that cannot usefully be straightened.

## How it is serviced

Never. A door that has been struck should have its bar inspected rather than just the skin repaired, because a deformed bar no longer performs.
