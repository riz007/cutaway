A box of activated charcoal between the fuel tank and the atmosphere. It exists because petrol evaporates, and evaporated petrol is both a pollutant and wasted fuel.

## What it does

Vapour from the tank — generated whenever fuel warms up or the car is refuelled — is routed into the canister, where the charcoal adsorbs the hydrocarbons and lets clean air out of the vent.

The charcoal would saturate if that were all. So while the engine runs, the ECU opens a purge valve that lets manifold vacuum draw fresh air backwards through the canister, stripping the stored hydrocarbons off the charcoal and pulling them into the <Part id="air.intake_manifold" /> to be burnt. The system regenerates itself continuously.

Because purging adds unmetered fuel, the ECU must compensate, which is why purge is scheduled rather than constant.

<Note>
The whole tank is a sealed pressure system, which is why a loose filler cap illuminates the engine light: the ECU pressure-tests the circuit and a missing seal reads as a large leak.
</Note>

## How it fails

Saturation from repeated over-filling at the pump, and stuck purge valves that cause rough idling or a fuel smell.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Stop filling at the pump's first click.
