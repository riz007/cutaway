A block of friction material on a steel backing plate, designed to be the part that wears out.

## What it does

It converts kinetic energy into heat by rubbing on the <Part id="braking.rotor_front" />. What matters is not simply how much friction it has, but how stable that friction is. A pad's coefficient of friction changes with temperature, and a road pad is formulated to be consistent from a cold first stop on a wet morning up to a hard descent — which is why fitting a race pad to a road car makes it worse, not better: those compounds only work hot.

Modern pads are non-asbestos organic or low-metallic composites: fibres, fillers, friction modifiers and binder resin. Composition is a balance between friction, wear, noise and how kind they are to the rotor.

Most have a wear indicator — a metal tab that squeals against the rotor at minimum thickness — and many have a shim and chamfer to suppress the vibration that causes brake squeal.

## How it fails

By wearing out, and by uneven wear where a <Part id="braking.caliper_front" /> guide pin has seized so only the inner pad works.

## How it is serviced

Measured at every service, replaced in axle sets, never one wheel alone.
