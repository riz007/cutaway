A single-wire network for devices too slow and too unimportant to justify a place on the <Part id="electrical.can_bus" />.

## What it does

Not everything needs CAN's speed or robustness. A mirror motor, a seat adjuster, a rain sensor, a wiper module or an alternator regulator exchange a few bytes occasionally and do not care about a millisecond of delay. Putting them on CAN would be expensive overkill.

LIN uses one wire plus the vehicle's own body as the return, and runs about forty times slower than CAN. It has a single master that polls each slave in a fixed schedule, so there is no arbitration to design and no collision to handle — the master simply asks and the slave answers.

The result is a network node that costs a fraction of a CAN node. A typical car has one CAN backbone and several small LIN sub-networks hanging off it, each managed by a module that bridges to CAN.

## How it fails

Because it is single-wire and unshielded, it is more susceptible to electrical noise and to corrosion than CAN. A failed slave typically stops responding, and the master reports it missing.

## How it is serviced

Not a service item. Diagnosed with a scan tool and an oscilloscope.
